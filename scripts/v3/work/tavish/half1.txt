
Tavish Gray is the head of the Gray family, tobacco planters at Caliga Hall near Rhodes, in Red Dead Redemption 2 (2018), set in 1899. His family has long feuded with the Braithwaites of Catherine Braithwaite. In chapter 3 he hires Arthur Morgan, John Marston and Javier Escuella to steal the Braithwaites' prized horses, which he says will fetch 5,000 dollars; the fence pays 700. Working for the other side, Arthur and Sean MacGuire burn his tobacco fields, and the Grays answer with an ambush in Rhodes, at which Tavish is not present. He is later found dead on a porch of Caliga Hall, beside a letter addressed to a Mr. Gray which says that the family's ancestor, Ross Gray, was a paid informer for the Duke of Cumberland. The game does not show how he died.
According to the Red Dead Wiki, he is voiced by Madison Arnold.
Key facts
He heads the Gray family of Caliga Hall, a tobacco plantation east of Rhodes; his relatives hold the sheriff's office and, according to Archibald MacGregor, own all the businesses in town.
In "Horse Flesh for Dinner", his only scene, he hires Arthur, John and Javier to steal Catherine Braithwaite's prized horses and promises 5,000 dollars; the fence Clay Davies pays 700.
In "The Fine Joys of Tobacco", Arthur and Sean burn his tobacco fields for the Braithwaites; the Grays' ambush in Rhodes follows in "A Short Walk in a Pretty Town", without him.
He is found dead on a porch at Caliga Hall, next to a historian's letter saying that the Gray ancestor was an informer for the Duke of Cumberland, not an exiled Jacobite.
His son Beau calls Sheriff Leigh Gray his uncle, while Dutch and Arthur call the sheriff Tavish's son.

Biography
Origins
The game gives no date or place of birth for Tavish Gray. In 1899 he heads the Gray family, the owners of Caliga Hall, a tobacco plantation in Bayou Nwa, in the state of Lemoyne, between Rhodes and Saint Denis. The Red Dead Wiki describes him as the family's patriarch and its eldest member. His son Beau Gray introduces himself to Arthur as the son of Tavish, the nephew of Sheriff Leigh Gray and the grandson of Murdo Gray. This makes Tavish the brother of the sheriff and the son of Murdo. Tavish speaks of his sons in the plural, and Beau mentions his brothers, but Beau is the only son named. Tavish also says that the Braithwaites killed his uncle.
The game is not consistent on this point. When Arthur meets Tavish, he says he has met his son, the sheriff, and nobody corrects him. Earlier, Dutch explains that Sheriff Gray got the gang into Caliga Hall by speaking to his father (see Quotes). The Red Dead Wiki records both versions and treats the mismatch as an inconsistency in the game. Beau's version, which also appears on the wiki's family pages, is the one this profile follows.
Tavish describes the Grays as an old Scottish family. He says his father taught him to work hard but fair, and that he taught the same to his sons. He also says the family has not recovered from the war as well as he had hoped, that some people in the area will always hold it back, and that it needs stability. The Rhodes deputy Archibald MacGregor tells Arthur that the Grays have lived at Caliga Hall for generations and own all the businesses in Rhodes, including the saloon. The family also holds the law in town through Sheriff Leigh Gray. According to the Red Dead Wiki, Caliga Hall predates the Civil War, the Grays built their fortune on tobacco and cotton grown by enslaved people, and that fortune declined after the abolition of slavery.
The Grays have been feuding with the Braithwaites of Braithwaite Manor, the family of Catherine Braithwaite, for longer than anyone in Rhodes can explain. Archibald says that each family believes the other stole a fortune from it long ago, and that he cannot say whether the story is true. The Red Dead Wiki dates the feud to 1806, when Lucille Braithwaite let Douglas Gray take her family's hidden gold; he used it, with gold taken from his own family, to fund abolitionist groups, and each family then accused the other of the theft. The wiki also reports that Joe Butler, a Confederate veteran in Rhodes, tells Arthur the Grays made most of their money before settling in Rhodes about fifty years earlier, and that he believes some of them spied for the Union during the war.
Red Dead Redemption 2 (1899)
Chapter 3: Clemens Point
After the move to Clemens Point, near Rhodes, Dutch van der Linde decides to play the Grays and the Braithwaites against each other and rob them both. Arthur Morgan helps Sheriff Leigh Gray recapture the Anderson boys in "The New South". In "American Distillation", the sheriff deputizes Dutch, Arthur and Bill Williamson to shut down a Braithwaite still. In "The Course of True Love I", Dutch sends Arthur to look around Caliga Hall; the guards let him in only because he says he has been deputized, and he meets Beau instead of Tavish. Dutch later tells Arthur that John Marston and Javier Escuella are at the Grays' place about the Braithwaites' prized horses.
Tavish appears in person in a single mission, "Horse Flesh for Dinner". Arthur joins John and Javier at the stables of Caliga Hall, where they are already talking with Tavish. John tells him that the gang lost money on a failed railway speculation out west. Tavish answers with his complaints about the war and the family's difficulties. He then blames Catherine Braithwaite and her sons for ruining the county and for killing his uncle. He says the Grays cannot be seen to get too close to the Braithwaites and that they have gold to pay. He calls John a Yankee; John replies that his own father came over from Scotland, and Tavish says that he is Scottish too. Asked about the Braithwaites, he insults them as peasants and mongrels.
Tavish then makes his offer. The Braithwaites' horses are prize animals that he values at five thousand dollars. A buyer in Clemens Cove will take them out of the state and pay half their value. He asks the three men to keep the Grays out of it in public, and tells them that the thoroughbreds are kept in the stables on the south side of the manor. John accepts. As they ride off, John calls him a crazy old fool, and Arthur says Tavish does not know what he is talking about, though a third of the price would still be worth it. At Braithwaite Manor, John tells a guard that they want to invest in the stables, the three men take three horses and fight their way off the estate. At Clemens Cove, the fence Clay Davies says he knows the horses are not theirs and offers 650 dollars, then pays 700. Arthur writes in his journal that the gang believed a story told by one of the Grays and felt like fools (see Quotes).
The order in which the missions are played changes the scene. According to the Red Dead Wiki, if "The Fine Joys of Tobacco" has already been played, the guards at the gate tell Arthur they have had trouble, Tavish tells John and Javier that the burning of his fields makes him suspicious, and John denies any part in it, saying he prefers smoking tobacco to burning it. If the horse theft comes first, Arthur warns on the ride out that Hosea and Sean, who are working the Braithwaites, had better keep their heads down. The wiki adds an optional exchange (player choice): if the player dismounts and speaks to Tavish before leaving, Arthur can either thank him for the job or mock the idea that anyone would pay five thousand dollars for stolen horses.
Working for Catherine Braithwaite, Arthur and Sean MacGuire set fire to Tavish's tobacco fields in "The Fine Joys of Tobacco". On the way, Sean says he has heard that security at the estate was doubled after the trouble at the Rhodes saloon. They get in by posing as a supply wagon. A guard named Hamish tells Sean that Mr. Gray has a heap of problems with another family trying to sabotage his livelihood. The guard is killed, and at nightfall Arthur and Sean douse the fields and a barn with moonshine, set them alight, fight off the guards and escape with horses from a payroll wagon.
Tavish takes no visible part in what follows. In "A Short Walk in a Pretty Town", Micah Bell tells Arthur that some of the Grays have spoken to Bill about a security job in Rhodes. Bill says the Grays think the Braithwaites burned the fields. The job is an ambush, and Sean is shot dead in the street. Sheriff Leigh Gray tells the gang that the family knows what it has been doing and that Rhodes is the Grays' town. He comes out of his office holding Bill hostage, with his deputies and Jock Gray, and Arthur and Micah kill them. Tavish does not appear in the mission. When Arthur gets back to camp, Jack Marston has been taken by the Braithwaites, and the gang burns Braithwaite Manor in "Blood Feuds, Ancient and Modern".
Death and the letter from Edinburgh
The game never shows Tavish's death and never states its cause. His body can be found on a porch of the main house at Caliga Hall, a point of interest called "Gray's Secret". He sits slumped forward in a wicker chair, his right hand on his left forearm, next to a small marble table holding a coffee pot and a cup. No weapon or wound is visible. The Red Dead Wiki's page on the point of interest says the body can be found once "Horse Flesh for Dinner" is completed, while the wiki's page on Tavish places his death some time after the Rhodes ambush.
A letter lies on the table beside him. It is dated April 3, 1895, comes from Malcolm Moffat, senior professor of history at Edinburgh University, and is addressed to "Mr. Gray". Moffat thanks him for his "long and well researched letter" about an ancestor who left Scotland for the colonies after the Jacobite rising of 1745, and admits he could not tell whether the man was his great-grandfather or great-great-grandfather. He writes that he is sorry to disappoint such a passionate "Scot in exile" as Mr. Gray calls himself.
Moffat has studied the spy networks of the Duke of Cumberland, who kept precise records. According to those records, the ancestor, Ross Gray, was "unquestionably a well-paid informer". He was paid in cattle and cash and was given land confiscated from his neighbor. Moffat writes that this explains why Ross Gray stayed in Scotland until 1755, ten years after the rising, the year some Jacobite supporters began reprisals against known informers in the Highlands. Moffat adds that his own ancestors include both rebels and loyalists, that descending from loyalists is nothing to be ashamed of, and invites Mr. Gray to visit the university. Arthur and John each write about the discovery in their journals (see Quotes).
Red Dead Redemption 2: the epilogue (1907)
According to the Red Dead Wiki, Tavish's body is still in its chair in 1907, unchanged and untouched, when John can visit Caliga Hall. The wiki adds that the Gray family still owns the estate, that workers still tend the tobacco fields and that guards still patrol at night. In Rhodes, Sheriff Harmon Thomas, elected after Leigh Gray's death, has pledged to rid the town of the influence of both families, according to the wiki. Simon Pearson, who now runs the general store in Rhodes, tells John that people in town still talk about the brutal end of the Grays and the Braithwaites.
Personality
Rockstar's character descriptions of September 2018 cover the Van der Linde gang and do not include him. He has a single scene, at the Caliga Hall stables in "Horse Flesh for Dinner". In it he speaks of a family still struggling since the war and in need of stability. He insists on his Scottish roots and on the value of hard, fair work. He wants the theft done without any public link to his family. He describes the Braithwaites with insults and slurs, and accuses them of ruining the county and killing his uncle (see Quotes). He values the stolen horses at 5,000 dollars; the fence pays 700 (see Biography).
Dutch sums up the Grays as "Scots... degenerates... drunkards", adds that they are the local law and calls them "Rich as Croesus". His son Beau says the Grays have been the Braithwaites' murderers for so long that nobody can explain why, "Beyond blind loyalty... and stupidity".
In his own words
"We're an old Scottish family. We work. My daddy taught that to me, I taught my sons. Work hard, but fair." (to John)
"We've got gold, Yankee... we've got gold." (to John, who asks what the family wants)
"I'm Scottish." (after John claims that his own father came over from Scotland)
"Just keep us away from this... publicly I mean."
Names
His first name is given by his son Beau, who introduces himself as the son of Tavish Gray. At the stables John calls him Mr. Gray, and the letter found beside his body opens with "Dear Mr. Gray"; in it the historian notes that his correspondent describes himself as a "Scot in exile". On the way to Caliga Hall in "The Fine Joys of Tobacco", Sean says that "Old Man Gray" has doubled security; the game does not name him further. Riding away from the stables, John calls him a "Crazy old fool". Dutch and Arthur speak of him as the sheriff's father (see Relationships, Leigh Gray).
Appearance
He is an elderly man, bald on top, with long gray hair on the sides, heavy dark eyebrows and a thick graying beard. At the stables he wears a dark suit over a white shirt.
