Arthur Morgan

Died 1899 Deceased Van der Linde gang Voiced by Roger Clark

By Joseph Lambert · Updated October 7, 2026

Arthur Morgan is the protagonist of Red Dead Redemption 2 (2018). Rockstar presented him as "Dutch's most dependable and capable enforcer since he was a boy", for whom "the outlaw life is all Arthur has ever known".

He is played by Roger Clark, whose performance won Best Performance at The Game Awards 2018.

Key facts

  • Taken in as a boy by Dutch van der Linde and Hosea Matthews; in 1899 he has been in the gang for about twenty years.
  • The player controls him for the six chapters of the main story, from the snow of Colter to Beaver Hollow.
  • Diagnosed with tuberculosis in 1899, he turns against Dutch and helps John Marston escape with his family.
  • He dies in 1899; the game has four endings, decided by his honor and by a final choice.
  • Roger Clark won Best Performance at The Game Awards 2018 for the role.

Biography

Origins

No birth year is given in the game. In 1899, Arthur tells Charles he has been with the gang for "twenty years, something like that. Since I was a boy", and Hosea says they found him at about thirteen, "maybe a little older". According to the official guide, he joined the gang at fourteen. Agent Milton later describes him as an "orphaned street kid" taken in by Dutch.

Arthur says Dutch taught him to read, as he taught John, and that Dutch and Hosea taught him most of what he knows. According to the official guide, he had a son, Isaac, with a waitress named Eliza; he sent them money until both were killed in a robbery. He was once engaged to Mary Gillis, now Mary Linton; in a letter returning his ring, she writes that his world "is not a world from which one can escape". Letters to the gang are addressed to an alias invented by Dutch, Tacitus Kilgore.

Red Dead Redemption 2 (1899)

Chapter 1: Colter

After the failed ferry robbery in Blackwater, the gang shelters in Colter, an abandoned mining camp in the snow. With Javier, Arthur finds John wounded by wolves. He takes part in the raid on an O'Driscoll camp in "Old Friends", then in the robbery of a train belonging to Leviticus Cornwall, which sets Cornwall on the gang's trail and puts the Pinkertons back on it.

Chapter 2: Horseshoe Overlook

Arthur helps free Sean MacGuire from bounty hunters and pulls a drunk Reverend Swanson off a railway bridge just before a train. For Leopold Strauss, he collects debts; in "Money Lending and Other Sins III", he beats a sick farmer, Thomas Downes, who coughs blood in his face. Agents Milton and Ross approach him while he is fishing with Jack and offer him a deal to give up Dutch; he refuses. He also helps Mary Linton look for her brother. In "The Sheep and the Goats", a shootout with Cornwall's men in Valentine forces the gang to move to Clemens Point.

Chapter 3: Clemens Point

The gang plays the Grays against the Braithwaites. In the Rhodes ambush, Sean is killed and Arthur shoots Sheriff Leigh Gray. Captured by Colm O'Driscoll, Arthur escapes wounded. He goes back to the Downes ranch to collect the debt from Thomas's widow, Edith. After the Braithwaites kidnap Jack, the gang burns their manor and moves into Shady Belle.

Chapter 4: Saint Denis

In Saint Denis, Arthur recovers Jack from Angelo Bronte; Dutch later kills Bronte and throws him to the alligators, which disturbs Arthur. He meets Chief Rains Fall and his son Eagle Flies, and helps them against Cornwall. The robbery of the Saint Denis bank is a Pinkerton trap: Hosea is killed by Agent Milton, Lenny dies, John is arrested, and the survivors flee by ship.

Chapter 5: Guarma

Shipwrecked on Guarma, the survivors side with Hercule Fontaine's rebels against Alberto Fussar; Arthur kills Fussar before they sail home. Back with the gang, Arthur collapses in Saint Denis, and a doctor diagnoses tuberculosis.

Chapter 6: Beaver Hollow

With Sadie, Arthur frees John from Sisika Penitentiary, against Dutch's wishes. He can forgive the last debts he is sent to collect, and throws Strauss out of the camp. He helps the Wapiti against the Army. At the oil fields, in "My Last Boy", Dutch leaves him behind; during the army payroll robbery, in "Our Best Selves", John falls from the train and Dutch leaves him for dead. Arthur breaks with Dutch.

The final mission and the four endings

In "Red Dead Redemption", Arthur rescues Abigail at Van Horn, where Agent Milton reveals that Micah Bell is the Pinkertons' informant before Abigail shoots him. Back at Beaver Hollow, Arthur and John confront Micah; Susan Grimshaw sides with them and is shot by Micah. The Pinkertons attack; the two men escape through the caves and ride off, then continue on foot when their horses are shot. On the mountain, Arthur chooses to help John get to safety, which raises his honor, or to go back for the gang's money, which lowers it. In both cases, he gives John his hat and satchel.

  • Helping John, high honor: Micah walks away; Arthur crawls to the edge of the cliff and dies watching the sunrise. A deer appears.
  • Helping John, low honor: Micah tells him "you're not better than me" and shoots him in the head. A coyote appears.
  • Going back for the money, high honor: Arthur and Micah fight with knives; Arthur wounds Micah's left eye and, when Micah tells him he is dying, answers "inside you're dead"; he dies at sunrise.
  • Going back for the money, low honor: Micah stabs him; after Dutch leaves, Micah stabs him again in the back.

In every ending, Dutch walks away. The game does not give a numerical honor threshold.

After his death

In the 1907 epilogue, the player controls John. Arthur's memory runs through it: "Arthur saved my life... more than once", John says, and Sadie tells him "we owe this to Arthur" as they hunt Micah down on Mount Hagen. In the ending credits, Mary Linton visits his grave. Arthur does not appear in Red Dead Redemption (2010), set later, nor in Red Dead Online.

As a playable character
  • An honor system tracks his actions. It changes some dialogue and scenes, the animal shown after his death (a buck or a coyote), and the outcome of the final mission.
  • Dead Eye slows time and lets him mark targets.
  • His weight changes with what the player makes him eat: underweight, he has less health and more stamina; overweight, the reverse. He can bathe, and his hair and beard grow in real time.
  • Once he is ill, his cores drain faster and food restores less.
  • He keeps a journal of sketches and notes, updated as the story advances.

Development and performance

A single protagonist

After the three protagonists of Grand Theft Auto V, Rockstar returned to a single lead. "Sticking with a single character felt more appropriate for the structure and narrative of a Western", Rockstar's Josh Bass told The Hollywood Reporter in September 2018; "this is Arthur's story." Producer Rob Nelson told Variety that the team "wanted to stick with Arthur through this story and see how he changes from the start".

Writing

Writer Dan Houser told GQ that Arthur's journey "is not about becoming a superhero, because he's almost one at the start", but that he "is going to be taken on a more intellectual roller coaster when his world view gets taken apart". To Vulture, he described a man "caught between the nastiness of nature and the brutality of encroaching industrialization", with "a feeling that his time may have come and gone".

Several ideas were cut. Arthur once had two love interests, but "we decided one of them didn't work", Houser told Vulture; according to Vulture, about five hours of the 65-hour game were cut. In a 2025 podcast interview, Houser said an early opening in which Arthur's baby died was dropped because "it was too tough in some ways" and made Arthur "very, very nasty" at the start. Roger Clark told Twinfinite that, after an early story event was cut, Arthur "came across as a pretty grumpy fellow", so the team went back and changed some scenes.

Roger Clark

Roger Clark was born in the United States in 1978 to an Irish mother and an Irish-American father, moved at twelve to just outside Sligo, in Ireland, and studied drama in Wales. He auditioned cold, without the script in advance, and was asked to wear cowboy boots. "My first day on the project was in August of 2013", he told The Hollywood Reporter; director of performance capture Rod Edge added that "the first scenes we did were with Roger". Clark describes the role as five years of work, "predominantly performance capture" rather than voice work in a booth.

His inspirations, he told The Hollywood Reporter, were Toshiro Mifune, John Wayne and John Marston, rather than Clint Eastwood's Man With No Name, because Arthur "needed to be a little more talkative". He told Twinfinite the aim was "a character who makes sense whether he's a complete bastard or if the player decides to choose the honorable path".

Reception

Awards

  • The Game Awards 2018: won Best Performance (Roger Clark).
  • BAFTA Games Awards 2019: nominated for Performer; won by Jeremy Davies (God of War).
  • D.I.C.E. Awards 2019: nominated for Outstanding Achievement in Character; won by Kratos (God of War).
  • New York Game Awards 2019: nominated for the Great White Way Award for Best Acting in a Game.

Critics and rankings

IGN noted "an infectious authenticity" in Clark's voice. Kotaku's Kirk Hamilton wrote that Arthur first struck him as "deliberately unremarkable", but that by the end he had become "a character in his own right". In Game Informer, Javy Gwaltney called Arthur's arc "a fantastic take on memento mori and how ruthlessly messy and complex redemption can be". In The New York Times, Peter Suderman described "a bad man with a good heart, because his choices are, in fact, your own".

GamesRadar+ ranked Arthur 29th among the 50 most iconic video game characters in 2021. In an April 2024 BAFTA public poll of more than 4,000 voters, he was voted the 11th most iconic video game character. According to The Irish Times, Arthur was "slow to warm to" for some players but has become one of Rockstar's best-received characters.

Trivia
  • "Tacitus Kilgore", the name on the gang's mail, is an alias invented by Dutch; in the epilogue, John's letters are addressed to Jim Milton.
  • Agent Milton puts the bounty on Arthur's head at 5,000 dollars.
  • Dan Houser told GQ he avoided Roger Clark on set so as not to hear his real voice: in some 20,000 to 30,000 lines, Clark never drops the accent.
  • Clark told Shacknews that his lines to his horse were recorded in a booth, and redone because the first take sounded "a little too erotic".
