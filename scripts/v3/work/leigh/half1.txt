
Leigh Johnson is the marshal of Armadillo, in New Austin, in Red Dead Redemption (2010), set in 1911. When John Marston asks him for help against Bill Williamson, John first helps him with his own problems. They track the outlaw Walton Lowe, wipe out a band of cattle rustlers at Pike's Basin and capture Norman Deek, Williamson's second-in-command, after a raid on Ridgewood Farm. When Bill's gang kidnaps Bonnie MacFarlane to free Deek, Johnson leads the posse to Tumbleweed. He then joins the assault on Fort Mercer with his deputies Jonah and Eli, but Bill has already left for Mexico.
In 1914, the last newspaper of the game reports that he has retired at 54, after 17 years as marshal. He is voiced by Anthony De Longis and also appears in Undead Nightmare.
Key facts
In "Political Realities in Armadillo", John offers to deal with Walton's gang before they discuss Williamson; together they follow Walton Lowe to Pleasance House.
In "Justice in Pike's Basin", he and his deputies wipe out the rustlers holding a rancher's cattle.
In "Spare the Rod, Spoil the Bandit", his posse finds the massacre at Ridgewood Farm and captures Norman Deek.
In "Hanging Bonnie MacFarlane", the exchange of Deek for Bonnie at Tumbleweed is a trap; John cuts Bonnie down from the gallows.
In "The Assault on Fort Mercer", he and his deputies take the fort with John, Dickens and Seth, but Bill has left for Mexico.
In Undead Nightmare, which is not canon, he sends John after his missing deputies in "Dinner for Two".

Biography
Origins
The games give no birth date for Leigh Johnson. An article published in 1914 gives his age as 54, which places his birth around 1860; the French-language Red Dead Wiki gives 1861. The same article says he served as marshal of Armadillo, in the Cholla Springs county of New Austin, for 17 years, which places the start of his service around 1897. In "Political Realities in Armadillo", when John says he does not want to be a policeman, Johnson answers that he did not want to be one either. In "Justice in Pike's Basin", he recalls that when he and others first settled in the region, people from Dade County seemed exotic to them, and that men can now come from the Midwest in six days.
Johnson was married. His wife, Priscilla Johnson, is buried in the cemetery of Coot's Chapel, in Cholla Springs. Her tombstone describes her as the wife of "Marshall Leigh Johnson", gives the date of her death as April 23, 1903, and carries the epitaph "Forever smiling in our hearts." It gives no cause of death. According to the Red Dead Wiki, Johnson never mentions her in the game. By 1911, he commands two deputies, Jonah and Eli, who ride with him on every mission.
Red Dead Redemption (1911)
In 1911, agents of the Bureau of Investigation hold John Marston's wife and son to force him to hunt down Bill Williamson, who holds Fort Mercer, in the neighboring county of Rio Bravo. According to the Red Dead Wiki, which dates the story from the issues of the Blackwater Ledger, John reaches Armadillo in early October 1911. Johnson appears in five missions, all in the first act. He gives each of them, from "Political Realities in Armadillo" to "The Assault on Fort Mercer", and takes no part in the events of Mexico or West Elizabeth.
Act I: New Austin
Johnson is involved before he meets John. In "Exodus in America", John's guide from Armadillo to Fort Mercer, a man named Jake, says the marshal hired him after receiving a telegram from important people in Blackwater who asked for a guide. In "Wild Horses, Tamed Passions", Drew MacFarlane says he has seen the telegram the marshal received from Blackwater, and that everyone knows who sent John. After John recovers at MacFarlane's Ranch, Bonnie MacFarlane advises him in "Obstacles in Our Path" to pay the marshal a visit about Williamson.
In "Political Realities in Armadillo", John walks into the marshal's office, where the deputy Jonah takes him for one of Williamson's men. The two are pointing guns at each other when Johnson comes in. He orders Jonah to lower his weapon, recognizes John as the man from Blackwater and sends Jonah out. When John says he has come to capture or kill Williamson, Johnson laughs. Williamson, he explains, is outside his jurisdiction, in the next county, and his gang has stayed away from the town. His job is to keep Armadillo safe, not to clean up all three counties. He then lists his problems: the railroad, which pays his salary, wants him to look away while it burns down settlements; cattle rustlers need dealing with near a box canyon; a gang keeps murdering homesteaders in the back country; and a band of drunk hoodlums in the saloon is threatening to shoot up the town.
John offers to deal with the hoodlums first. Johnson identifies their leader as Walton Lowe, whose gang robs the stagecoaches coming in and out of town; he admits he has let them drink in the saloon rather than rob travelers. Walton rides out as they arrive, and the two men follow him. Johnson refuses to shoot him on the spot: that is not how the law works, he says, and a living prisoner can talk. He adds that Walton's gang is growing and operates across Cholla Springs and Gaptooth Ridge.
At Pleasance House, the gang spots them and Johnson orders John to take cover and work his way up the hill. Depending on the player, John kills Walton or wounds him in the leg. Johnson compliments John's shooting, invites him to call on him next time he is in town, and rides off with his prisoner. According to the Red Dead Wiki's page on Walton Lowe, the marshal accepts his death if John kills him, and takes him to the Armadillo jail if he is captured. Either way, Blackwater Ledger No. 54 reports that Walton Lowe, aged 30, has died by gunshot and will be missed by nobody.
In "Justice in Pike's Basin", John finds Johnson struggling with the office's new telephone line, Armadillo 731. Johnson tells him that things have got away from him and that he no longer understands them. Eli runs in to report that four men, the Bollard Twins and two Mexicans by his account, are rounding up Mr. Gulch's livestock in the canyon. Johnson asks John to ride with them, and John agrees on the condition that the marshal helps him in return. On the way, Johnson reveals that federal agents in Blackwater have sent him a telegram saying John works for the government. He tells John he dislikes this federal interference, and mocks the idea that city men who have never worked a farm can understand New Austin.
At Pike's Basin, Johnson has his men dismount, warns them they will probably be outnumbered, and tells them to take the rustlers alive if they can and to kill them if they cannot. Snipers are waiting for them. At a fork, Johnson takes the right-hand path and the deputies the left; the player chooses which side John follows. The posse wipes out the gang and frees Mr. Gulch's ranch hands, who thank the marshal; he tells them to get the cattle back safely. When John raises Williamson again, Johnson promises to do what he can. Eli then reports that Nigel West Dickens, the tonic seller, has gone missing. Johnson dismisses him as a peddler of narcotics, and remarks to John that the town cares more about a missing salesman than about the gang they have just wiped out.
In "Women and Cattle", Bonnie tells John she has heard from Johnson that he plans to settle in the area and build a life there; John says those are not his plans. In "Spare the Rod, Spoil the Bandit", Johnson and his deputies are getting their rifles ready when John arrives, and the marshal asks him for another gun. For months, he explains, a group of bandits has been getting drunk and murdering settlers. The night before, they attacked a large property near Ridgewood. Eli adds that they burned the place and killed its people, and that one survivor walked into town that morning. A posse is gathering near Ridgewood. John agrees to ride, and Johnson warns him the job will be bloody. Besides John, Eli and Jonah, the mission page lists the lawman Dell Hopkins in the posse.
On the way, Johnson mentions that he has heard John found West Dickens in the meantime. Vultures lead the posse to two camps full of corpses; at the second, the fire is still smoking. Johnson says that a pack of weak men and cowards is the most dangerous kind, and notes that they have spilled a lot of blood fighting Walton's gang and the rustlers, and that a man grows more evil when he is running scared. When Jonah asks John about his own past in a gang, Johnson says that killing and stealing are never right. At Ridgewood Farm, he senses something is wrong and has the posse search the grounds. He tells John to shoot open the boarded-up barn door, behind which they find several bodies, one of them hanging from the rafters, and a woman who says her family is held hostage in the farmhouse. The posse storms the house and frees the survivors.
One of the survivors, a woman, accuses the marshal of failing to protect them and calls him a man on a government payroll. Johnson orders the posse to mount up and offers fifty dollars to whoever kills the gang's leader; the woman answers that it is not about money. On the road, Eli wonders whether the bandits are heading for Fort Mercer, and Johnson agrees that it looks like the work of Williamson's men. Johnson spots someone on the cliffs: Williamson, who warns John off, then sends his men to attack. Johnson orders everyone into a shed; according to the mission page, they take cover in the ruins of Mercer Station. After the fight, Jonah finds Norman Deek, Williamson's second in command, still alive. Johnson greets Deek by name, says he is glad to see him again, thanks John, says Deek will help them get to Bill, and has him hogtied and taken to jail.
Issue No. 55 of the Blackwater Ledger reports the massacre. According to its article "Massacre at Ridgewood Farm", Armadillo lawmen stormed the farmhouse, killed the criminals and freed the survivors. The paper adds that it is not yet confirmed whether the Williamson gang was responsible, and that if not, the beleaguered Marshal Leigh Johnson has another serious problem on his hands.
In "Hanging Bonnie MacFarlane", Drew MacFarlane is at the marshal's office: Bonnie has not been seen since the previous afternoon, and he suspects John. Johnson tries to calm him. A rider from Williamson's gang calls the marshal out into the street. Johnson orders him off his horse and threatens to shoot, but the rider says the gang holds Bonnie and wants Norman Deek released in exchange, at Tumbleweed, within a couple of hours. Johnson tells him the law does not bargain with outlaws, then asks where she is. Once the rider has gone, Johnson asks what they should do; John answers that they must do as he says. Jonah carries the hogtied Deek out of the office, and Johnson keeps John at his side.
On the ride, Johnson blames the kidnapping on federal interference; when John says again that he does not work for the government, Johnson answers that he knows who sent him. He says the federal men promise peace and civilization but bring only trouble and taxes. He calls Deek a glorified errand boy, mean enough to be a second in command but too cowardly and stupid to lead. Asked about their destination, he explains that Tumbleweed was a real town until the railroad reached Armadillo and bypassed it; its people left, and it is now a haunt of thieves, smugglers and bandits, and a common place for lynchings. He asks John whether he is taking advantage of the MacFarlanes, whom he describes as vulnerable. He says he trusts John, but has doubts given his past, and calls it madness to send an outlaw to do a lawman's work.
The two men then argue about the law. Johnson holds that there must be rules, that without laws men are animals, and that a child who misbehaves should be punished harder each time. John replies that laws are of little use if people do not understand them, and asks why university men in the East should decide how people live in Armadillo; Johnson concedes he may be right on that point. At Tumbleweed, Johnson has John lead Deek into the middle of town for the exchange, warning that he doubts the gang will play fair. The gang's Kent Gallaway refuses any deal and shoots Deek; Johnson shouts that it is a trap and orders the posse to take cover and find Bonnie. John saves her from the noose. According to the Red Dead Wiki, Johnson then takes her back to Armadillo. Blackwater Ledger No. 55 reports that she escaped hanging after a shootout between Armadillo lawmen and Williamson's gang, and announces the death of Norman Deek, aged 34, in the gun battle.
"The Assault on Fort Mercer" brings together John, Johnson, his deputies Eli and Jonah, Nigel West Dickens and Seth Briars, who is already inside the fort; Irish supplied the Gatling gun but misses the fight. According to the mission page, an unnamed deputy also takes part. On a hilltop overlooking the fort, John tells Johnson that all he cares about is Williamson, and the marshal agrees that the man is a cold-blooded killer. The deputies shut John in the back of Dickens's wagon, which carries the Gatling gun. Seth welcomes the wagon at the gate, Dickens raps on its side, and John opens fire, clearing the way for the lawmen. Johnson leads them through the fort, regroups them, and, finding no trace of Williamson, orders them to tear the place apart. When Dickens returns to the gate, Johnson has it opened and locked behind him. Reinforcements arrive, and John stops them with the Gatling gun.
Williamson is not in the fort. Jonah brings in a captured gang member, who says Bill left for Mexico the morning before; John concludes that he has gone to join Javier Escuella, and Irish offers to take him south. Johnson apologizes to John and says he supposes John will now be heading to Mexico. John says goodbye to Dickens, then to the marshal. According to the mission page, this is Johnson's last appearance in Red Dead Redemption. Blackwater Ledger No. 56 reports the battle in "Shoot Out at Fort Mercer": it seems to mark the end of the Williamson gang, though not of Williamson himself, whose body has not been found and who is believed to have fled to Mexico. The article says that Marshal Leigh Johnson led the raid and was commended for his bravery.
The 1914 newspaper
Act III: West Elizabeth
Johnson's later life is told in issue No. 213 of the Blackwater Ledger & Sentinel, dated 1914, which becomes available after the final mission, "The Last Enemy That Shall Be Destroyed", with Jack Marston as the playable character. Its article "Marshall Johnson of Armadillo Retires" calls him legendary and reports that he has retired at 54, after 17 years as town marshal. It credits him with bringing down some of New Austin's most notorious outlaws: Walton's Gang, the Two Crows Boys, One-Lung Lester Riley, the Bollard Twins and Wildcat Willie. The people of Armadillo held a rowdy farewell party for him at the town's saloon, during which he arrested three men and was reportedly the last man standing at the end of the night. Asked about his plans, he said he would move as far away from Armadillo as humanly possible. He is alive at the end of the game.
Undead Nightmare (non-canon)
Undead Nightmare, an expansion released in October 2010, is not part of the series' canon. In it, Armadillo is overrun by the undead and its survivors hide on the rooftops. Johnson appears in the side mission "Dinner for Two", available once John has completed "Curious Tales from Blackwater". According to the mission page, John enters the marshal's office as an undead prisoner in a cell is about to devour a living one; when the living man is bitten, Johnson shoots them both. He tells John that the town has nearly been overrun and that the world has fallen into chaos. According to the Red Dead Wiki's page on Armadillo, he says the undead keep attacking, then disappear for a while before coming back.
Johnson has sent his deputies, Jonah and Eli, out into the town, and they have not come back; the Red Dead Wiki's page on the expansion says they have been missing for a few hours. When John offers to look for them, Johnson hands him a sawed-off shotgun, which belonged to Jonah according to the character page. John finds the two deputies at the stables behind the saloon: both have turned, and Eli is eating Jonah. They attack John, who kills them. Back at the office, Johnson asks whether it was quick and whether they died happy, and John jokes that they died on a full stomach, or at least one of them did. Johnson accepts the news, and John keeps the shotgun. Johnson plays no further part in the story.
Personality
The Red Dead Wiki quotes the Game of the Year Edition guide of Red Dead Redemption: "He is a 50-year-old ageing cynic. This good ol' boy would be considered a southern gentleman. He is good with words, but he is slowly being defeated by the pressures facing him in the West. He has been showing signs of cracking up, but he still tries his best to maintain a dignified facade."
In the game he defines his job as keeping Armadillo safe, not the three counties of New Austin. He tolerates some outlaws when the alternative is worse: he lets Walton's men drink in the saloon rather than have them rob travelers. He wants prisoners alive, because a live man can talk. He calls younger men boy or son and addresses John as Mr. Marston, then John. He mocks his deputy Eli and Nigel West Dickens with dry, sarcastic remarks (see Relationships). He distrusts the federal government and the men it sends from the East.
In his own words
To John, at their first meeting in "Political Realities in Armadillo": "Well, I ain't happy, but I also ain't suicidal." Then: "My cause is to keep this town from turning into a living hell for the folks who live here. Whole world has problems, mister, and I'm here, doing what I can." At his desk, in "Justice in Pike's Basin": "I remember when we first got here, we used to consider people from Dade County to be exotic. Now guys can get here from the Midwest, and they can do it in six days." He says this while failing to get through on the new telephone line in his office.
On the law, in "Spare the Rod, Spoil the Bandit": "Killing and thieving's never right, boy, no matter how you dress it up." On the road to Tumbleweed, in "Hanging Bonnie MacFarlane": "Criminals are like weeds, Marston. Quick as you stomp one out, another one sprouts up in its place."
Names and title
Most characters call him the Marshal or Marshal Johnson. Jonah answers him with "Yes, sir, Mr. Johnson, sir", and Eli also calls him Mr. Johnson. Bill's messenger hails him from the street with "Oh, Mister Marshal". His full name is spoken once by Jake, the guide who escorts John at the start of the game: "it was the Marshal who hired me. Leigh Johnson". Wikipedia's summary of the game calls him a U.S. Marshal; the Red Dead Wiki calls him the district marshal. In 1914, the Blackwater Ledger & Sentinel spells the title Marshall in its headline, calls him a "Legendary Armadillo Marshal" and says he served as "town marshal" for 17 years.
Appearance
He is a man in his fifties with a lined face, pale eyes, gray hair and a full gray beard that ends in a long goatee; according to the Red Dead Wiki, the beard has a slight yellow tinge. He wears a wide-brimmed gray hat with a brown band, a light shirt with a dark necktie and a dark patterned vest, with the marshal's badge on his chest.
