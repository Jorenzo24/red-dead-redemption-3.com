Harold MacDougal

Anthropologist Yale University Blackwater, 1911 Voiced by Joe Ochman

By Joseph Lambert · Updated October 22, 2026

Harold MacDougal is a Yale anthropology professor in Red Dead Redemption (2010), set in 1911. On a sabbatical he admits was forced on him, he studies the Native Americans of West Elizabeth from his room at the Blackwater Hotel. Edgar Ross and Archer Fordham bring him the Bureau's wounded informant, Nastas, and Ross tells John Marston to help him. MacDougal rides with John and Nastas towards the hideout of Dutch van der Linde at Cochinay, then to a meeting with Dutch's men at Bearclaw Camp, where Nastas is shot dead.

When Dutch's gang attacks his hotel, John gets him across the rooftops and escorts him to the train at Manzanita Post, and he leaves for Yale. In 1914, a newspaper reports that Yale has expelled him after he beat a colleague with a croquet mallet. In Undead Nightmare, which is not canon, he is killed by an undead Nastas. He is voiced by Joe Ochman.

Key facts

  • In "Bear One Another's Burdens", Ross, Fordham and John bring the wounded Nastas to his room in Blackwater, and Fordham asks him to find out what Nastas knows about Dutch.
  • In "At Home with Dutch", he rides with John and Nastas to the cliffs below Cochinay, then turns back to Blackwater.
  • In "For Purely Scientific Purposes", the meeting with Dutch's men at Bearclaw Camp turns into an ambush in which Nastas is killed; John brings MacDougal back to Blackwater.
  • In "The Prodigal Son Returns (To Yale)", Dutch's gang besieges his hotel; John frees him on the roof and puts him on the train at Manzanita Post.
  • In 1914, the Blackwater Ledger & Sentinel reports his expulsion from Yale.
  • In "Curious Tales from Blackwater" (Undead Nightmare), he is killed by an undead Nastas.

Biography

Origins

Harold MacDougal is a professor of anthropology at Yale University, in New Haven, Connecticut. He tells John Marston that he comes from Connecticut, teaches at Yale and writes books. According to a newspaper article of 1899, his academic work centers on race and civilization, and he is the author of a book titled "The Scourge of Crossbreeding". In 1911, he tells John that he has spent eight years trying to work out what he calls the nature of the savage soul, meaning why some societies rise and others do not. He adds that he argued about it at Yale with a colleague named Fortisque and that the dispute caused a minor scandal. The games give no date or place of birth.

He uses cocaine, which he injects with a syringe and offers to share with John at their second meeting, saying it helps his thinking. In "For Purely Scientific Purposes", he blames part of his agitation on what he calls his standard narcotic impulses. The Red Dead Wiki says he discovered the drug at Yale; no scene shows it. The wiki also reports three remarks it places in "For Purely Scientific Purposes", although they are absent from the mission's transcript: anthropology has fascinated him since he was a boy, his father did not consider it a real profession, and he is living on his inheritance during his time away from Yale. In the transcripts, MacDougal admits that his sabbatical was somewhat forced on him by circumstance. Archer Fordham gives another reason on arrival in Blackwater (see Quotes).

Red Dead Redemption 2 (1899)

MacDougal does not appear in Red Dead Redemption 2. He is named in a newspaper article, and according to the Red Dead Wiki one of his books appears as a prop (see Trivia).

Chapter 6: Beaver Hollow

The article "Prominent Yale Scientist Speaks Out" runs in the Blackwater Ledger No. 71, the New Hanover Gazette No. 36 and the Saint Denis Times No. 52, issues released after "The King's Son". Its headline reads "MIXING OF RACES DISCOURAGED." and its subheading "AMERICAN SOCIETY AT RISK." It reports that MacDougal presented a paper titled "Savage Minds: The Racial Continuum Between Animal and God" at a symposium the week before. The paper argues that some races are predisposed to build higher functioning societies than others. The article quotes him: "Savages cannot construct buildings that rise to the heavens, but we have". It describes him speaking "in an agitated and excited manner". It closes on a rebuttal: his colleague Benjamin Fortescue "asserted that MacDougal has no empirical evidence to support his wild claims".

Red Dead Redemption (1911)

Act III: West Elizabeth

In 1911, MacDougal is living in Blackwater, West Elizabeth, where he studies the region's Native Americans. According to Fordham, he also helps the Bureau of Investigation deal with the local tribes. The Red Dead Wiki dates his missions to November 1911 from the order of the Blackwater Ledger issues. At that point the Bureau still holds John Marston's wife and son, and Edgar Ross now wants John to find Dutch van der Linde. MacDougal appears in four missions of the third act: "Bear One Another's Burdens" and the three missions of his own strand.

At the end of "Bear One Another's Burdens", Ross, Fordham and John bring the Bureau's informant, Nastas, back from the wreck of the Serendipity, beaten and wounded in the leg. Ross decides to leave him with MacDougal to see what the professor can get out of him, since he says he cannot communicate with Native Americans himself. They stop at what Fordham calls MacDougal's office. Fordham asks the professor to find out what Nastas knows about Dutch and promises to send a doctor. MacDougal says he will do his best. Before leaving, Ross presents him to John as a good friend of the government and suggests that John help him with his study.

"At Home with Dutch" opens with MacDougal at his microscope. He has compared the blood of Native Americans and white men of matching height, weight and age, and found no difference. He admits that the result contradicts his last book. He asks John whether he is of Norse stock and calls him a white man with a savage spirit. When Nastas comes in, MacDougal addresses him slowly, in images about slippers, rabbits and stars. Nastas answers in plain English that he grew up on a reservation and went to school. He then says that he knows where Dutch's gang is hiding, in the hills of Cochinay, and that young men from the reservation are following Dutch. MacDougal welcomes the news as proof of his own theory and rides out with John and Nastas.

On the ride, MacDougal explains that he does not want Dutch dead: a white man living among Native Americans interests him as a case study. He says Ross told him about John's history with Dutch while he was under the influence. If the player has not yet unlocked the Manifest Destiny achievement, the three men pass hunters chasing buffalo. Nastas warns that the white man's hunting will wipe the herds out; MacDougal replies that extinction is a myth and that hunting will make the species stronger. In the woods of Tall Trees he grows nervous and says he rarely leaves his room. At the foot of the cliffs he admits he has no head for heights, leaves John and Nastas to climb, and rides back to Blackwater.

John watches Dutch's camp through a scout's binoculars until Dutch spots him and fires; the bullet hits the binoculars and John falls backwards, knocked out. John wakes up in Blackwater, with MacDougal holding smelling salts under his nose. The professor first claims that he and Nastas carried John down the mountain. When Nastas grumbles, he corrects himself: Nastas heard the shots and carried John down alone, while he himself improvised the escape plan. Nastas then reveals that friends of his are with Dutch, and MacDougal proposes meeting them to keep them out of trouble. When Nastas recalls that this was once all his people's land, MacDougal answers that white men brought them civilization. Nastas tells him that, for a wise man, he is very stupid.

In "For Purely Scientific Purposes", MacDougal greets John in a state of agitation and says he has finally solved that riddle (see Origins). Nastas has arranged a meeting with some of Dutch's men at the Bear Claw cabin, in Bearclaw Camp. MacDougal sees it as a meeting of minds and wonders whether he could take skin samples from the soles of their feet. On the way, a grizzly bear crosses the path and MacDougal shouts at John to shoot it. He and John both open fire and kill the bear. Nastas remarks that the animal showed no aggression and would have moved on if they had not fired first.

Three of Dutch's men wait in the cabin. MacDougal tells them they come in peace and that violence is not the answer. One of them, Enepay, blames people like him for what has been done to the tribes and orders the visitors to raise their hands. When Nastas protests that this was not the agreement, Enepay calls him a treacherous snake and shoots him in the head. John kills Enepay, Kosumi and Hinto, then the gang members outside, while MacDougal begs him not to leave him behind. On the ride back, two more groups of Dutch's men attack them. MacDougal concludes that the savage mind cannot be civilized, even though Nastas and the blood samples had led him to the opposite view. John objects that they were ambushed by Dutch's gang and that it is too soon to draw conclusions about an entire people. Back in Blackwater, MacDougal says he needs a syringe and will probably never sleep again, then thanks John for saving his life.

In "The Prodigal Son Returns (To Yale)", John finds MacDougal packing his bags. The professor says he is not cut out for this, calls his attackers savages and injects himself with cocaine to steady his nerves. A shot rings out; according to the mission page, Dutch has shot out a window. Dutch calls to John from outside, says he will kill both of them, and asks John to send out the academic so that his men can show him what they think of anthropology. John jokes that he will hand MacDougal over, then takes him through the neighboring room, where a couple protests. MacDougal goes up to the roof first and is seized there. According to the mission page, John climbs a ladder to reach him and finds one of Dutch's men, Nashoba, holding him at gunpoint; John shoots him. John clears the gunmen on the surrounding rooftops, and the two men ride out of town.

On the road to the Manzanita Post station, John fights off two more ambushes. MacDougal says he came west hoping to record the romance of the last days of the Old West and found only people killing each other. At the station, he declares his research complete and expects a prize in New Haven for it. He says goodbye to John, hugs him and wishes him luck. According to the mission page, he is in tears as he boards the train back to Yale. He does not appear again in Red Dead Redemption. Afterwards, Ross and the army storm Dutch's hideout at Cochinay, where Dutch lets himself fall from a cliff.

Issue No. 59 of the Blackwater Ledger, available after "The Prodigal Son Returns (To Yale)", covers the attack under the headline "BLACKWATER BESET BY SAVAGES. NOTED ANTHROPOLOGIST SURVIVES". It presents MacDougal as "a prominent academic on sabbatical from Yale University", says he "was apparently ambushed at his room in the Blackwater Hotel", and credits him and "an unnamed gunman" with subduing the attackers. It names Dutch van der Linde as the suspected leader of the band. The same issue carries a notice in its Miscellany column: "Nastas (Surname Unknown), of Indian descent, has passed away at Bear Claw Cabin in Tall Trees. May you find God, my dear friend. Prof. Harold MacDougal Esq."

The 1914 newspaper article

Act III: West Elizabeth

Issue No. 213 of the Blackwater Ledger & Sentinel, dated 1914 and bought by Jack Marston after John's death, carries the article "Anthropologist Turns Savage", subtitled "New Haven in Shock". It reports that MacDougal "has been expelled from the university following a drunken brawl with fellow academic Lionel Fortisque". It recalls that he "spent six months in West Elizabeth in 1911" and "published a damning thesis last year on the impossibility of taming the savage mind". Citing a Connecticut newspaper, it says the two men argued "over Polynesian cannibals at a garden party" and that MacDougal beat Fortisque "with a croquet mallet".

The article continues: "the agitated professor fled to the roof of Woolsey Hall where he removed his clothes and threatened to jump if somebody did not bring him a plate of Beef Wellington and a bottle of '94 Claret." University officials told the paper that he "had become increasingly unhinged in recent months". He was already on probation over allegations that "he had attempted to pay a groundskeeper to remove the top of his skull so that he could photograph a living brain". The article gives no month for these events. It is the last mention of MacDougal in the series; nothing is said of what becomes of him afterwards.

Undead Nightmare (non-canon)

Undead Nightmare, an expansion released in 2010, is not part of the series' canon. MacDougal appears at the start of "Curious Tales from Blackwater". John comes to Blackwater looking for a doctor for Abigail and Jack, and finds the town empty except for MacDougal, standing unarmed in the street. The professor says he came back for more research and admits that he has no idea what is happening. John suggests they look for survivors together. MacDougal first wants to get his bag, down a deserted street; the mission page calls it a medical bag. According to the Red Dead Wiki's character page, he had left his bag with Nastas. A few steps on, an undead Nastas throws himself on him and devours him while John watches.

The player can then kill both undead or avoid them. According to the mission page, if they are left alone, they may turn up again later in the mission, either in town while John clears Blackwater, where they must be dealt with to finish it, or just outside the cemetery once it is cleansed. The character page adds that the undead MacDougal can be hogtied instead of killed, and that outside the cemetery the player may also leave him to roam.

Personality

The Red Dead Wiki quotes Rockstar's description of the character: "A highly-strung anthropology professor, on a forced sabbatical from Yale." It also quotes the Game of the Year Edition guide, which presents him as "very much, an east coast turn-of-the-century man" and "an optimist who loves the American way of life and speaks in large metaphors." According to the guide, he is on sabbatical from Yale, "writing a book about Native Americans and the last days of the old west". The same guide says he has "taken his study of opium a little too seriously" and that his sabbatical may have been forced. In the missions, the drug he names is cocaine (see Origins).

He repeats himself ("Good day, good day", "but, but, but") and stammers when he is nervous or excited. He avoids danger whenever he can (see Biography). He sorts peoples into civilized and savage. He uses the word savage for Native Americans throughout. His conclusions about Native Americans shift from one mission to the next (see Biography).

In his own words

When John tells him, in "At Home with Dutch", not to read too much into his past with Dutch: "It's my job to read too much into everything, dear boy." In the dark woods of Tall Trees, when John asks whether he has ever seen trees: "Good lord, no. I rarely leave my room. I explore with the mind, Mr. Marston." At the foot of the cliffs: "I'm afraid I don't really have much of a head for heights. More of a head for highs..."

Back in Blackwater, after Nastas has carried John down the mountain: "I'm more of a planner than a man of action." On the ride home from Bearclaw Camp, in "For Purely Scientific Purposes": "I must say... cocaine can be a cruel mistress."

Names

John and Nastas call him Professor, Edgar Ross calls him Professor MacDougal, and Archer Fordham simply MacDougal. Dutch van der Linde never uses his name: he calls him "your friend there the professor" and asks John to send out "that academic". The Game of the Year guide, as quoted by the Red Dead Wiki, calls him Dr. MacDougal, and the French-language Red Dead Wiki gives Professeur as his nickname. He signs his notice in Issue No. 59 of the Blackwater Ledger "Prof. Harold MacDougal Esq." He calls John Mr. Marston, "dear boy" and once "old bean"; held hostage on the roof of the Blackwater Hotel, he calls him by his first name: "John! Help me, John!"

Appearance

He has untidy reddish-blond hair, a thin mustache curled at the ends, stubble and round wire-rimmed spectacles. In Blackwater he wears a white shirt with a loose bow tie. According to the Red Dead Wiki, his boots are close to the US Army model but lace up at the front.

Relationships

John Marston

Edgar Ross sends John to help the professor with his study (see Biography). MacDougal sums up John's history with Dutch as a tale of naivety and betrayal.

John mocks him openly: he calls him a fool in front of Nastas and jokes about handing him over to Dutch's men (see Quotes). John nonetheless protects him at Bearclaw Camp, on the ride back and in Blackwater, then escorts him to his train (see Biography).

Nastas

Nastas is the Bureau's informant, whom Ross leaves with MacDougal after he is found wounded on the Serendipity (see Biography). Nastas knows the way to Dutch's hideout and arranges the meeting at Bearclaw Camp. The two men argue on the rides (see Biography and Quotes).

MacDougal says he is relieved to have Nastas with him on the way to the meeting Nastas arranged at Bearclaw Camp, where Nastas is shot dead. He then publishes a notice on his death in the Blackwater Ledger (see Biography).

Edgar Ross

Ross leaves the wounded Nastas with the professor and introduces him to John (see Biography). He has also told MacDougal about John's past with Dutch (see Biography).

Archer Fordham

Fordham describes MacDougal as an anthropologist who helps the Bureau with the Native Americans of the area, and gives his own account of why he left Yale (see Quotes). He is the one who tasks him with questioning Nastas (see Biography).

Dutch van der Linde

MacDougal does not want Dutch dead and studies him as a case (see Biography and Quotes). Dutch's men ambush him at Bearclaw Camp, and Dutch then besieges his hotel (see Biography).

Enepay, Kosumi, Hinto and Nashoba

Members of Dutch's gang. Enepay, Kosumi and Hinto meet MacDougal at Bearclaw Camp, where Enepay rejects his words of peace and shoots Nastas (see Biography). According to the mission pages, Nashoba is the gunman who holds MacDougal hostage on the roof of the Blackwater Hotel; the transcript calls him only an outlaw.

Lionel Fortisque

A fellow academic at Yale, the subject of a minor scandal with MacDougal and, in 1914, of the croquet mallet attack (see Biography).

Benjamin Fortescue

The colleague who, according to the Red Dead Redemption 2 article "Prominent Yale Scientist Speaks Out", criticized MacDougal's 1899 paper for lacking empirical evidence (see Biography). The newspapers of the two games spell the names Fortescue and Fortisque and give different first names; the games never say whether they are the same man.

The newlyweds

A couple in the room next to MacDougal's at the Blackwater Hotel. When he and John burst in to reach the roof, MacDougal greets them politely and the husband protests (see Biography).

His father

The game never shows him. According to the Red Dead Wiki, he did not consider anthropology a real profession (see Origins).

Research, weapons and horses

Research and writings

He works from his room at the Blackwater Hotel, which Fordham calls his office, with a microscope and shelves of books. His method in 1911 is to compare blood samples from Native Americans and white men of the same height, weight and age (see Biography). The Bureau uses him as its intermediary with the Native Americans of the region (see Relationships). His known writings, all mentioned in newspapers of the two games:

  • The Scourge of Crossbreeding, a book published before 1899, named in the Red Dead Redemption 2 article "Prominent Yale Scientist Speaks Out".
  • Savage Minds: The Racial Continuum Between Animal and God, a paper presented to colleagues at a symposium in 1899 (see Biography).
  • A thesis on the impossibility of taming the savage mind, published in 1913 and left untitled by the 1914 Blackwater Ledger & Sentinel (see Biography).

Weapons

The Red Dead Wiki lists a Cattleman Revolver. According to the mission page, "For Purely Scientific Purposes" is the only mission in which he uses his gun, against the grizzly bear (see Biography). He does not fire in any other mission.

Horses

The Red Dead Wiki lists two mounts, an Ardennais and a Cleveland Bay. He rides with John and Nastas to Tall Trees and to Bearclaw Camp, and with John to the station at Manzanita Post (see the Missions table). Nastas brings the horses round to the front of the building before the second ride. The mission page of "For Purely Scientific Purposes" notes, in its trivia, a speed quirk: in that mission the two men's mounts outrun even the American Standardbred and the War Horse.

In the game

"At Home with Dutch" and "For Purely Scientific Purposes" fail if John assaults, kills or abandons MacDougal or Nastas, or kills their horses. "The Prodigal Son Returns (To Yale)" fails if John abandons him, if he is killed while held hostage on the roof, or if John harms him while he is held hostage.

Missions

MacDougal has a strand of three story missions in the third act of Red Dead Redemption, numbered 44 to 46 in the story order. It opens after Edgar Ross's "Bear One Another's Burdens", the first mission of the West Elizabeth part, and runs alongside Ross's own strand. The last of the three, "The Prodigal Son Returns (To Yale)", leads to Ross's "And You Will Know the Truth". He also opens a mission of the Undead Nightmare expansion.

Development and performance

Casting

Wikipedia credits Joe Ochman as Professor Harold MacDougal in Red Dead Redemption (2010), where the same credit line also lists him as a government clerk. Its article on Undead Nightmare, the expansion released later in 2010, names Ochman as MacDougal again; his own Wikipedia page marks that second performance as uncredited. Wikipedia describes Ochman as an American actor who works mostly in voice-over for animation, film and video games. His other roles listed there include Jiminy Cricket in Kingdom Hearts III, Will A. Zeppeli in JoJo's Bizarre Adventure, Zeno Zoldyck in Hunter x Hunter and William Price in Life Is Strange (2015).

After the game

In a written interview published on a Red Dead Wiki user blog on November 10, 2011, K. Harrison Sweeney, the voice of Irish, said he had been talking with Anthony De Longis, Joe Ochman and Frank Noon about possibly appearing in From the Trailer to the Grave, a comedy he had written and planned to shoot with actors from Red Dead Redemption.

Reception

Coverage

On November 10, 2018, WhatCulture published a list by Ewan Paterson of eighteen easter eggs and references in Red Dead Redemption 2. Entry 17 is Harold MacDougal. Paterson calls him "an eccentric (read: racist drug addict)" from the closing stages of the first game, a Yale professor who studies the Native American population "only in the interest of furthering his own bigoted perspective". He notes that MacDougal does not appear in person in the sequel but is mentioned in dialogue and in the game's printed material.

Other media and legacy

Multiplayer

MacDougal became a playable character model in the multiplayer mode of Red Dead Redemption with the Liars and Cheats Pack, detailed by Rockstar on August 27, 2010 and released on September 21, 2010. Players select him in the Damnation section of the Outfitter, with Abraham Reyes, Agustin Allende, Bill Williamson, Dutch van der Linde, Edgar Ross and Irish. The versions of Red Dead Redemption released on PlayStation 4 and Nintendo Switch on August 17, 2023, then on PC on October 29, 2024, do not include the multiplayer mode.

Undead Nightmare artwork

According to the Red Dead Wiki, promotional artwork of an undead MacDougal was made for Undead Nightmare. He also appears on one of the expansion's loading screens: undead, beside the Blackwater Saloon, in pursuit of someone who stays out of frame.

Trivia
  • The title "The Prodigal Son Returns (To Yale)" refers to the Parable of the Prodigal Son, told in the Gospel of Luke (15:11-32).
  • According to the mission page of "The Prodigal Son Returns (To Yale)", MacDougal can be seen packing a Bible into his bag. Later in the game, the same room belongs to Chu Fuk Choi, a stranger, according to the same page. MacDougal's briefcase stays in it; the Bible does not.
  • In Red Dead Redemption 2, according to the Red Dead Wiki, a copy of "The Scourge of Crossbreeding" disguises the lever that opens the secret door of the bookcase in the Saint Denis fence's shop, during the stranger mission "Help a Brother Out" (chapter 4).
  • Woolsey Hall, the building whose roof MacDougal climbs in the 1914 article, is a real building: according to Wikipedia, it is Yale's main auditorium, opened in 1901 for the university's bicentennial.
  • The 1914 issue that reports his expulsion from Yale also runs an article titled "Shocking Report Claims Cocaine Bad for Health".
