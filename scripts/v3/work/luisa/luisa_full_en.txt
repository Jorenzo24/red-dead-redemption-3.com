
Luisa Fortuna is a young Mexican teacher and rebel in Red Dead Redemption (2010), set in 1911. She lives with her family at Campo Mirada, in Nuevo Paraíso, and fights for Abraham Reyes, the leader of the revolt against Colonel Agustin Allende. John Marston and Landon Ricketts free her from an army jail in the caves near El Matadero. She then gives John the rebel missions of the second act: her sister Miranda's escort to the docks, Reyes's rescue at El Presidio, an ambush on an army convoy and the attack on Vincente de Santa at Sepulcro.
She believes that she and Reyes are married in their hearts; Reyes confuses her name and tells John he will never marry a peasant. In Escalera, she runs at the soldiers about to execute Reyes and is shot dead. She is voiced by Francesca Galeas.
Key facts
In "Landon Ricketts Rides Again", John and Ricketts break her out of an army jail in the caves near El Matadero.
In "My Sister's Keeper", she asks John to take her sister Miranda to the docks while her family leaves Campo Mirada.
In "Must a Savior Die?", she drives John to El Presidio, where he saves Reyes from a firing squad.
In "Father Abraham", after the army kills her father, she has John blow up an army convoy near the Frontera Bridge.
In "Captain De Santa's Downfall", she sends John and her men to Sepulcro to stop De Santa's massacre.
In "An Appointed Time", she is shot dead in Escalera trying to save Reyes.

Biography
Origins
The dialogue of Red Dead Redemption gives no birth date for Luisa Fortuna. According to the Red Dead Wiki, which cites the game's Game of the Year guidebook, she was born in 1892, which makes her 19 in 1911. She is Mexican and lives in Nuevo Paraíso, the Mexican province south of New Austin. Her family's house is at Campo Mirada, a homestead in the Perdido region; John Marston describes her to Abraham Reyes as the girl from Campo Mirada. The family has four other members in the game: an unnamed father, an unnamed mother, a brother named Emilio and a sister named Miranda. According to the Red Dead Wiki, Emilio is the eldest of the three children and Miranda the youngest. Miranda says that Emilio works as a driver and owns a stagecoach. Luisa describes the Fortunas as a close family.
Landon Ricketts presents her as a teacher. According to the Red Dead Wiki, which again cites the guidebook, she is training to become a schoolteacher in 1911 and taught herself to read, write and speak English as a child. The wiki's page on her father adds that he taught her to read and write Spanish. Of her family's standing, she says herself that she is little more than a peasant girl. She is also a rebel: she supports the uprising that Reyes leads against Colonel Agustin Allende, the military governor of the province. Ricketts tells John that she is close to the rebel leader, and Victor, one of Reyes's rebels, says in "Captain De Santa's Downfall" that she fights as well as any man.
Red Dead Redemption (1911)
In 1911, agents of the Bureau of Investigation hold John Marston's wife and son to force him to hunt down his former companions Bill Williamson and Javier Escuella. At the end of the first act, the trail takes John across the border into Nuevo Paraíso, where Allende's army and Reyes's rebels are fighting each other. Luisa appears in seven missions of the second act. Ricketts gives the first two, she gives the next four from Campo Mirada, and she dies in the last mission of the act, given by Reyes.
Act II: Nuevo Paraíso
Luisa is first mentioned in "Landon Ricketts Rides Again". At the hotel in Chuparosa, Ricketts tells John that the army is holding the sister of Emilio, the man he has been questioning about Escuella. On the way, he explains that the army wants her because she is a rebel close to Reyes, and that her looks are usually enough to interest Allende. A butcher named Carlos, in El Matadero, confirms that she is still held in the caves above the village and distracts the guards. Ricketts and John fight their way in, and Ricketts blows open the door of her cell with dynamite. She is barely alive, and Ricketts carries her out unconscious while John covers him. They escape on horses that Carlos has left at the cave mouth.
Near Diez Coronas, Carlos meets them and takes Luisa away for medical care. Before she leaves, she thanks the two men and calls them friends of the people of the land. Ricketts asks whether Javier Escuella was among the men holding her. She does not think so, but she remembers hearing the name in prison, from bad people. Ricketts takes it as a sign that Javier is still in Mexico.
In "The Mexican Wagon Train", she runs into the Chuparosa hotel while Ricketts and John are drinking. She reports that Allende is sending more men to their deaths without trial, among them a writer named Castillo and a local official whose only crime was refusing to evict smallholders who were late with their taxes. Ricketts jokes that for once he agrees with Allende, she protests, and he says he was joking and asks where the prisoners are. She answers that they are near Escalera, and thanks both men. According to the mission page, she then walks into the Chuparosa church and prays at the altar. On the trail, Ricketts tells John she is angry, and at the end of the mission he advises John to talk to her, because she is well connected on the Mexican side.
"My Sister's Keeper" opens at Campo Mirada as the family packs to leave the house. Luisa introduces John to her parents as the gringo who saved her, and her father thanks him in Spanish and in halting English. Luisa announces that the revolution is coming and that, with Reyes, anything is possible. Her parents and her brother are leaving for the hills, she says, and her sister has to flee because of the way the army treats women. Luisa herself stays. When John calls her noble but naive, she answers that she would rather be dead than a cynic like him, then adds that she knows he is not really like that, since he saved her.
Her father urges her to find someone to take Miranda to the port, because the boat leaves at nightfall. Luisa asks John for one more favor: drive her sister to the docks, from where she will go to work for a kind man in the Yucatán. She says Miranda is too young for revolution. John agrees and drives Emilio's stagecoach, with Miranda beside him, through several army checkpoints to the docks of Puerto Cuchillo. Miranda thanks him and leaves before sundown. Luisa does not take part in the ride.
At the start of "Must a Savior Die?", John finds Luisa in tears on the porch of the house, which has burned down in the meantime; according to the Red Dead Wiki, the army set it on fire. She says she weeps for her country rather than for herself: Reyes has been captured. He was riding to meet her at Roca de Madera, which she calls a very romantic spot, when an army patrol ambushed him, and he is held at El Presidio. She tells John that in their hearts they are already married, but that his family does not approve of a girl as poor as she is, and that Reyes does not care about such snobbery. She says she will rescue him or die trying. John suggests they ride together to the fort and work out a plan, and she kisses him on the cheek.
Luisa drives the coach to El Presidio, north of Campo Mirada. She points John to a partly collapsed wall he can climb, warns him that the guards will kill both men if they see him, and tells him to bring Reyes to friends who wait for her by the river. John kills the executioner from a sniper's nest, frees Reyes and rides out with him behind a rebel guide. On the way, Reyes calls her the love of his life, then asks John how he knows his young lover Laura; John corrects him. Reyes recognizes her only when he sees her at the river landing. She tells John that the revolution will live on thanks to him and cries out for Mexico. Reyes asks her to come with him and sings her a love song in which he again calls her Laura.
"Father Abraham" takes place after the battle of Chuparosa, where Reyes's rebels saved John from a firing squad. On his way to meet Luisa, John is stopped by rebels who take his gun and walk him to her campfire. She apologizes, saying they cannot be too careful. She then tells him that her father was killed the day before: the army accused him of treason, cut out his heart and fed it to the dogs. She blames Allende, and says he then took the honor of two young girls. She insists that her father's death must mean something. She explains that Allende has sent for reinforcements and that Reyes personally asked for John to stop them before they reach Escalera, by ambushing them on the old trail. John objects that he has a family to worry about. She answers that she has lost her father, that her mother is in the United States, that her sister has fled, and that she has nothing left but the cause. John agrees out of respect for her loss.
They set off for the Frontera Bridge, John either beside her on the wagon or on his own horse. On the way, Luisa says that her father's death must make them fight harder to win power for Reyes, whom she calls a true revolutionary who fights out of love rather than hate. She has heard that he killed nearly a hundred soldiers at Chuparosa to save John. She tells John that she still cannot forgive him for helping Allende and what he did to the rebels. They argue about revolution: she says there can never be a revolution without blood and that Mexico, after fighting off the Spanish, the French and the Americans, has only fought itself since independence; John answers that he has lost faith in power. She thanks him for helping her mother and Miranda escape, regrets not sending her father with them, and says they know nothing yet of his death. In the mission itself, John drove only Miranda. She asks John whether he has children, and he names his son Jack.
She explains that the convoy must not be robbed but destroyed, with everyone in it, because Reyes wants to send a message. Her men have no experience with explosives, so John places five charges on the road with the demolition team and mans the detonator on the ridge while Luisa watches for the wagons. The convoy is destroyed. Luisa thanks John, says her father would be proud, and returns to Campo Mirada with her rebels, promising to repay the favor.
In "Captain De Santa's Downfall", John finds her at the rebels' camp (Campo Mirada, according to the mission page) as her men load their rifles. She tells him that Captain Vincente de Santa has been sent to oversee a massacre at Sepulcro and asks him to kill him and the rest of Allende's followers. She sends her men with John and stays at the camp. At Sepulcro, John captures De Santa, who is then shot by John or by the rebels, at the player's choice (see Vincente de Santa).
Luisa does not appear in "The Gates of El Presidio", but she is discussed in its opening scene at Agave Viejo, where John walks in on Reyes with another woman. When John asks what Luisa would say, Reyes first asks who he means. He then recommends her to John, laughs at the idea that she thinks she is his wife, and says he will never marry a peasant, because he means to become president of Mexico (see Abraham Reyes).
"An Appointed Time" is the last mission of the act. The army has executed rebels in Escalera and captured Reyes. John walks through the town as the townspeople and rebels fight the soldiers, and finds Reyes being beaten in the square by Major Raul Zubieta and his men before his execution. John draws his gun and tells them to let Reyes go. Luisa then rushes at the soldiers with a knife, shouting in Spanish to leave her man alone, and the soldiers guarding Reyes shoot her dead. John kills two of them, then kills Zubieta in a duel and cuts Reyes loose. The Red Dead Wiki gives other versions of the shooting: its character page says Zubieta and his two men shoot her three times in the chest, its mission page says two soldiers fire, and its page on Zubieta says she was trying to stab him.
Luisa dies in Escalera in 1911, on the day the town falls to the rebels. After the battle, Allende and Bill Williamson die on the road out of town. Reyes proclaims that Mexico is his and thanks John, who answers that the victory also belongs to those who gave their lives, people like Luisa. Reyes calls her brave and says she will be missed, then asks who she was. John reminds him that she was his peasant girl and wife to be. Reyes promises that a day will be named after her and calls it Laura's Day. When John corrects him again, Reyes says he also knew a Laura, and turns back to politics.
Undead Nightmare (non-canon)
Undead Nightmare, an expansion released in 2010, is not part of the series' canon, and Luisa has no role in its story. According to the Red Dead Wiki, an ordinary undead uses her character model. The wiki says this undead is first met in Escalera, and that the gold cross necklace Luisa wears throughout the main game becomes visible on it when the player comes close and uses Dead Eye.
Personality
When it added her artwork to the official Red Dead Redemption website, Rockstar described her on its Newswire on May 3, 2010: "Luisa Fortuna is a 19-year old Mexican teacher and revolutionary, who knows how to handle a sidearm." On the road to Sepulcro, Victor Melendez, one of the rebels she commands, tells John: "She can fight as well as any man."
In the transcripts, she speaks Spanish with the rebels and her family and English with John and Ricketts, whom she calls Mr. Marston and Mr. Ricketts. She first calls him John during "Father Abraham" and greets him by that name in "Captain De Santa's Downfall". She speaks of Reyes as Abraham or Abraham Reyes, and of the revolution as a duty owed to the people and to her dead father. She frames it through Mexican history: the wars against Spain, France and the United States, and the province renamed Nuevo Paraíso (New Paradise) after the Spanish left. According to the mission page of "The Mexican Wagon Train", she can be seen walking into the Chuparosa church after the opening scene and praying at the altar.
In her own words
To John, as her family leaves Campo Mirada in "My Sister's Keeper": "Don't worry about me, Mr. Marston. I am living in history. I am not afraid to die." On the wagon ride to the Frontera Bridge in "Father Abraham", when John asks what comes after the fighting: "Revolutions are always won with blood." A few lines later, when he says that nobody is ever free: "Be honest with yourself and you will be free." As the army convoy reaches the charges: "Every time Allende hits us, we hit him back harder."
Names
Fortuna is the Spanish word for fortune or luck. Reyes calls her Laura: he asks John how he knows "my young lover, Laura" in "Must a Savior Die?", then sings "Laura, dame la fuerza para luchar" (Laura, give me the strength to fight) as they ride off together. After her death, in "An Appointed Time", he promises her a "Laura's Day" and, corrected by John, says he knew a Laura as well. When he recognizes her at the river, he calls her "Mi amiga" (my friend). The French-language Red Dead Wiki lists Laura as her nickname; the English-language wiki lists Laura and Peasent (sic), both attributed to Reyes.
She calls herself "little more than a peasant girl" when she explains why Reyes's family does not approve of them. Reyes, speaking of her to John at Agave Viejo, lumps her with "These peasant girls".
Appearance
She is a young woman with dark hair tied back. She wears a loose white blouse trimmed with blue at the neckline and sleeves. According to the Red Dead Wiki, she wears a gold cross on a beaded necklace throughout the game.
Relationships
Abraham Reyes
The rebel leader she loves and serves. She describes him to John as a true revolutionary who puts others first and fights out of love rather than hate, and says that with him anything is possible. She tells John they are already married in their hearts, but that his family cannot accept a peasant girl (see Quotes). He was captured on his way to meet her, and she sends John to free him at El Presidio (see Biography). In "Father Abraham", she passes on his orders and says the rebels will fight harder to win power for him.
Reyes confuses her name (see Names). At Agave Viejo, in "The Gates of El Presidio", he tells John that she was incredible, recommends her to him and laughs at the idea of marrying a peasant (see Quotes). She dies in Escalera trying to save him, and afterwards he cannot remember who she was (see Biography).
John Marston
John and Ricketts free her from the caves near El Matadero (see Biography); she thanks them as friends of the people of this land. In "My Sister's Keeper", John calls her naive and she answers that she would rather die than be a cynic like him, then says she knows he is not really like that (see Quotes). She kisses his cheek when he agrees to help free Reyes.
In "Father Abraham", she tells him she cannot forgive him for having helped Allende against the rebels, and that refusing to choose a side is a choice; John replies that Allende betrayed him. On the same ride, she asks about his family: he has a son at home and mentions a daughter who died, and he tells her the boy's name, Jack. After her death, John reminds Reyes of people like Luisa who gave their lives (see Quotes).
Landon Ricketts
Ricketts learns of her capture from her brother Emilio and rides with John to free her (see Biography). On the way, he describes her as a good woman and a teacher, and swears to make her captors pay if they touch her.
In "The Mexican Wagon Train", she runs into the Chuparosa hotel looking for him, to report the prisoners Allende is sending to their deaths. When he jokes that for once he agrees with the colonel, she protests and he says he was joking (see Quotes). At the border, he tells John to talk to her, as she is well connected in Mexico and will help him.
Agustin Allende
The governor whose army holds her in the caves near El Matadero (see Biography). She blames him for the executions of untried prisoners and for her father's death, calls him pure evil and says she will one day cut his heart out (see Quotes). He is killed on the road out of Escalera in the mission in which she dies (see Biography).
Vincente de Santa
She learns that the captain has been sent to oversee a massacre at Sepulcro, calls him an animal and vermin, and sends John there with her men to kill him and Allende's other followers (see Biography). She stays behind at the rebel camp.
Javier Escuella
Just after her rescue, Ricketts asks her whether Javier was one of the men holding her. She does not think so, but says she heard the name in prison, from bad people.
Jack Marston
She never meets him. In "Father Abraham", she asks John whether his son is looking after his wife and what the boy is called; John answers Jack, and says he is not much younger than her.
Emilio Fortuna
Her brother. In "Landon Ricketts Rides Again", he is talking with Ricketts at the Chuparosa hotel and answers only in Spanish. The Red Dead Wiki describes him as her older brother and a stagecoach driver; Miranda says he works as a driver, and she and John take his stagecoach. Luisa first says her parents and her brother are leaving for the hills, then, in "Father Abraham", that Emilio will stay and fight like her.
Miranda Fortuna
Her sister, younger according to the Red Dead Wiki. Luisa sends her to work for a kind man in the Yucatán, saying she is too young for revolution, and asks John to take her to the docks (see Biography). In "Father Abraham", Luisa thanks John for helping Miranda escape.
Her father
His name is not given in the game; the Red Dead Wiki calls him Mr. Fortuna. At Campo Mirada, he thanks John in English for saving his daughter and apologizes for his English. His death at the hands of the army is told under Biography. Luisa tells John she wishes she had sent him away with her mother and sister.
Her mother
Her name is not given in the game; the Red Dead Wiki calls her Mrs. Fortuna. She weeps on the porch at Campo Mirada as Miranda prepares to leave. In "My Sister's Keeper", Luisa says her parents are heading to the hills; in "Father Abraham", she says her mother is in the United States and thanks John for helping her mother and Miranda escape. Luisa adds that neither knows of her father's death.
Carlos
The butcher of El Matadero who helps Ricketts and John reach her cell by distracting the guards (see Biography). He thanks God when he sees her alive, and she leaves with him at the end of the mission.
Victor Melendez
One of her rebels, who guides John to Sepulcro on her orders. On the way, he praises her fighting (see Personality) and explains that women and even children must become soldiers if the rebels are to win.
Raul Zubieta
The army major who is about to execute Reyes in Escalera when she runs at his men with a knife. She is shot dead, and John kills Zubieta in a duel (see Biography).
Weapons and gameplay
The Red Dead Wiki lists a Cattleman Revolver and a Hunting Knife as her weapons. She has a knife in hand when she runs at the soldiers in Escalera (see Biography). According to the Red Dead Wiki, she is one of three major characters who never ride a horse, with Agustin Allende and Nigel West Dickens; at the end of "Landon Ricketts Rides Again", she leaves with Carlos, who, according to his wiki page, takes her home on horseback. She drives the wagon in "Must a Savior Die?", with John riding shotgun; according to the mission page, she can crash it if the player watches the journey instead of skipping it. In "Father Abraham", John chooses between riding in her wagon and following on his own horse.
In "Father Abraham", she says that her rebels have no experience with explosives: John marks the spots for the dynamite on the road and mans the detonator, while she watches for the convoy from the ridge and orders her men to their positions in Spanish. In "Captain De Santa's Downfall", she sends her men with John and stays at the camp. The missions she takes part in fail if John kills her ("Landon Ricketts Rides Again"), assaults or kills her ("Must a Savior Die?", "Father Abraham", "Captain De Santa's Downfall", where hurting any rebel also counts) or abandons her ("Must a Savior Die?"). Killing the horses that pull her wagon also fails "Must a Savior Die?" and "Father Abraham". According to the mission page of "An Appointed Time", her body cannot be looted. The missions she gives are listed in the Missions table.
Missions
Luisa appears in seven story missions of Red Dead Redemption, all in the second act, set in Nuevo Paraíso. Her own strand has four missions, which begin at Campo Mirada or, once the house has burned, at the rebel camp beside it. It becomes available after "Landon Ricketts Rides Again", given by Landon Ricketts, and "Civilization, at Any Price", given by Vincente de Santa. She is also discussed in one mission given by Abraham Reyes.

Development and performance
Casting
Luisa Fortuna appears in Red Dead Redemption, released in May 2010. The Red Dead Wiki credits Francesca Galeas as her voice, and the game's credits on IMDb list Galeas in the role. No interview in which Galeas discusses Red Dead Redemption was found. No source consulted names a performer for Luisa's motion capture, and Wikipedia's article on the game does not mention the character.
Design and promotion
On May 3, 2010, two weeks before the game's release, Rockstar presented on its Newswire two character artworks newly added to the official Red Dead Redemption website, one of Luisa and one of Irish. The post adds that both characters feature in the site's "People of the West" updates and in the game's character videos, and that the artworks are available as desktops, backgrounds, icons and avatars on the Downloads page. The French-language Red Dead Wiki notes that she is one of the backgrounds of the official site. According to the Red Dead Wiki, a pre-release version of the game gave her a more worn face, dark yellow teeth and unkempt hair. The Red Dead Wiki's page on the game's cut content does not mention her.
Reception
Character rankings
WhatCulture ranked her fifth in "5 Most Memorable Red Dead Redemption Characters (Aside From John Marston)", by David Michael Hughes, published on November 16, 2013. The entry opens: "Luisa Fortuna is a rebel who openly opposes Colonel Allende and is willing to suffer or die for the revolution." Hughes writes that "Luisa is nothing if not extremely brave", and that "Luisa sacrifices herself for Reyes, the man who cannot even recall her very name and only valued her for sexual pleasure." He adds that what she died for was not worth it, in his view, because Reyes becomes as bad a dictator as the man he overthrows.
Academic study
In "The Mustang Diaries: A Study of Mexico in Red Dead Redemption 1", published in the Revista Panamericana de Comunicación (vol. 4, no. 2, 2022), Javier Aldape, of Texas A&M University, describes her as "a staunch Reyista who firmly believes Abraham is the true savior of the country". In the same passage, he writes that Reyes forgets his followers' names. Aldape also notes, without naming her, that Reyes cares more about the corridos sung about him than about the soldaderas, the women fighters who die for his cause.
Other media and legacy
Multiplayer
Luisa became a playable character model in the multiplayer mode of Red Dead Redemption with the Liars and Cheats Pack, detailed by Rockstar on August 27, 2010 and released on September 21, 2010. The pack added 15 characters from the story, as well as Poker, Liar's Dice, horse races and the Stronghold mode. Players select her in the Redemption section of the Outfitter, alongside John, Abigail, Jack, Bonnie MacFarlane, Leigh Johnson, Nigel West Dickens and Seth Briars. According to the Red Dead Wiki, she wears a dress throughout the story, while her multiplayer model is given chaps, a change also made to Abigail's model. Her family's house at Campo Mirada, which burns in the story, is still standing in multiplayer, according to the wiki.
The versions of Red Dead Redemption released on PlayStation 4 and Nintendo Switch on August 17, 2023, then on PC on October 29, 2024, contain the story and Undead Nightmare but not the multiplayer mode, so the Luisa model is not available in them.
Marketing
According to the French-language Red Dead Wiki, an image of Luisa was one of the background images of Rockstar's official website for Red Dead Redemption.
Later games
Luisa does not appear in Red Dead Redemption 2 or in Red Dead Online. The Red Dead Wiki lists her only in Red Dead Redemption, and as a character model in Undead Nightmare (see Biography).
Trivia
According to the Red Dead Wiki, Luisa never rides a horse in the game. The wiki names only two other major characters in the same case: Agustin Allende and Nigel West Dickens.
According to their mission pages, "Landon Ricketts Rides Again" fails if the player kills Luisa, and "Must a Savior Die?", "Father Abraham" and "Captain De Santa's Downfall" fail if the player attacks or kills her. "Must a Savior Die?" also fails if the player abandons her.
According to the mission page of "Must a Savior Die?", if the player watches the coach ride to El Presidio instead of skipping it, Luisa can crash the vehicle, which fails the mission.
According to the mission page of "Father Abraham", Luisa calls out when to set off the charges, but her timing is not always right. The page says she can also criticize John's handling of the explosives even after a direct hit (noted on PlayStation 3).
According to the same page, "Father Abraham" is the first mission in which John says his son's name aloud.
Luisa names a writer, Castillo, among the prisoners in "The Mexican Wagon Train" (the mission page spells it Castilla). According to the mission page, none of the four prisoners it names at the border seems to be that writer or the local official she mentions.
According to the mission page of "An Appointed Time", Luisa falls on her back when she is shot, but lies face down once the cutscene ends. Her body cannot be looted.
The cemetery of Sepulcro has a grave in the name of Alejandro Fortuna, who died on August 14, 1909; its epitaph reads "He died for his country." according to the Red Dead Wiki's list of the graves. No dialogue links him to Luisa's family.
