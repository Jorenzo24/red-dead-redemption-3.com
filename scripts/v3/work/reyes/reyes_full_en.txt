
Abraham Reyes is the leader of the rebels of Nuevo Paraíso, in Mexico, in Red Dead Redemption (2010), set in 1911. The son of a nobleman, he leads a revolt against the province's governor, Colonel Agustin Allende, and the government of President Ignacio Sanchez. John Marston frees him from a firing squad at El Presidio after Luisa Fortuna tells him of his capture, and Reyes's rebels save John from his own execution in Chuparosa. In exchange for John's help, Reyes's spies track down Javier Escuella, and the rebels take El Presidio, then Escalera, where Allende and Bill Williamson die, shot by John or by Reyes.
In 1914, the last newspaper of the game reports that President Reyes denies being a tyrant after troops shot thousands of peaceful protesters south of Mexico City. He is voiced by Josh Segarra. He also appears in the non-canon expansion Undead Nightmare, as the undead John must kill to end the plague.
Key facts
In "Must a Savior Die?", John shoots his executioner at El Presidio and rides him to Luisa.
In "Cowards Die Many Times", Reyes and his rebels save John from execution in Chuparosa, where De Santa has betrayed him.
In "The Great Mexican Train Robbery", he and John rob an army supply train in Chuparosa.
In "The Gates of El Presidio", his rebels storm the fort so that John can reach Javier Escuella.
In "An Appointed Time", the rebels take Escalera; Luisa is killed trying to save him, and Allende and Bill die on the road.
In 1914, the Blackwater Ledger calls him President Reyes; in Undead Nightmare, which is not canon, John kills him as an undead in "A Civilized Man".

Biography
Origins
Red Dead Redemption gives no birth date for Abraham Reyes. His background comes from an article of the Blackwater Ledger, "Another Civil War in Mexico?", printed in issue No. 56. It describes him as a nobleman's son "born in a ranch just outside the capital", Mexico City. He was schooled in Mexico and in Europe. The paper adds that he was expelled from a Swiss boarding school "after an incident with a chamber maid". Neither of his parents is named. His mother is dead by 1911: Reyes says she would turn in her grave if he married a peasant girl. According to Luisa Fortuna, his family does not approve of her as a match for him.
Reyes himself tells John Marston a little more about his youth. On the ride to El Presidio, he says that when Javier Escuella was making a name for himself in the province, he was still travelling in Europe and living in Madrid with the ambassador's wife; he presents it as a time before his rebellious urges took hold. According to the Red Dead Wiki, he also says in "The Great Mexican Train Robbery" that his nursemaid used to tell him that one day everybody would know him; the line does not appear in the wiki's transcript of that mission.
The newspapers of 1911 set the political scene. Mexico is ruled by General Ignacio Sanchez, who, according to issue No. 53, "seized power last year in a bloody coup in which he killed his own brother". Issue No. 55 reports riots, train hijackings by rebel bands and government death squads, all played down by the government. Issue No. 56 then names Reyes as the leader of a band of rebels that has "seriously challenged government rule" in Nuevo Paraíso and the surrounding areas. He promises to "bring Mexico into the twentieth century, and improve conditions for the poor". The same article says he wants Mexico to play a leading part in world affairs, through an alliance with the Habsburg and Ottoman empires. His rebels are based at Agave Viejo, a ranch in the Perdido region of Nuevo Paraíso.
Red Dead Redemption (1911)
In 1911, agents of the Bureau of Investigation hold John Marston's wife and son to force him to hunt down his former companions Bill Williamson and Javier Escuella. The trail leads John to Nuevo Paraíso, a Mexican province governed from Escalera by Colonel Agustin Allende in the name of Sanchez. John works for both sides: for Allende and Captain Vincente de Santa, then for Luisa Fortuna and the rebels. Government officers describe Reyes to him before they meet (see Missions). Reyes is on screen in five missions of the second act, all in 1911, three of which he gives himself.
Act II: Nuevo Paraíso
In "Must a Savior Die?", John finds Luisa crying in front of her family's burned house. Reyes was riding to meet her at Roca de Madera when an army patrol ambushed him. He is now held at El Presidio, an army fort to the north, and is about to be executed. Luisa drives John there in a coach. John climbs over a broken section of the wall and reaches a sniper's nest. In the courtyard, an officer tells Reyes that he will pay for his treason and for wanting to overthrow Allende, then forms the firing squad. John shoots the executioner with a Rolling Block Rifle, kills the soldiers and cuts Reyes free. Reyes asks for a horse and rides out behind John. A rebel guide leads them to Luisa, who waits with friends at a landing on the San Luis river.
When John says that Luisa sent him, Reyes does not react to her name at first, then calls her the love of his life. Learning John's name, he says he had taken him for the American working for Allende and had planned to shoot him in the back. He then calls Luisa "Laura", and John corrects him. Reyes promises a reward: money, women, or Luisa if John wants her. John answers that he is only here for two men. Reyes celebrates his escape as if he had beaten a hundred soldiers, which John points out was his own work. At the river, Reyes greets Luisa and tells John that he is like a brother and that his people need such a man. He invites John to his ranch at Agave Viejo and promises to help him find his two men, if John helps win the people their freedom in return. He leaves with Luisa, singing to her and calling her Laura again.
In "Cowards Die Many Times", Allende tells John that Bill and Javier have been captured in Chuparosa, and De Santa takes him there. In the church, a soldier knocks John out. He comes to as his head is plunged into a horse trough, and De Santa's men prepare to execute him. Reyes and his rebels attack the town at that moment, and Reyes kills the man about to shoot John. He calls John over, cuts his ropes and tells him it was lucky that he arrived in time; John answers that they are now even. Reyes sends him to find his weapons. The rebels fight the soldiers in the streets, and John kills Captain Espinoza in the town hall. Bill and Javier were never in Chuparosa.
After the battle, Reyes gathers his rebels in the town and gives a speech. He tells them that the day has shown the regime's days are numbered, and promises a free republic justly governed by fine men. He warns them that the work is not finished, and that they will fight on, day and night, until local tyrants like Allende are gone and their followers put to the sword. He ends with cries of long live the revolution and long live Mexico, which the crowd takes up.
"The Great Mexican Train Robbery" begins at Agave Viejo. Reyes greets John as the American bandit turned bounty hunter who is about to win the Mexican revolution. He climbs on a stump to rouse his men, then tells John that their energy feeds him and that he and his people were sent to each other. He promises to be a fair and wise ruler because he knows the people and the land. When John answers with sarcasm, Reyes says sarcasm is beneath him and proposes a robbery for a good cause. On the ride, he asks John whether he has really come to Mexico to murder his two best friends. Pressed by John, he admits that he does not know exactly where Bill and Javier are, only that they are in Mexico, and that his best men are looking for them.
Reyes asks how John met the two men. John tells him about the gang of Dutch van der Linde, who robbed the rich, believed the system of power was rotten and thought change could only come through brutal means. Reyes asks whether Dutch was a revolutionary like him, says he likes the sound of this other violent idealist and asks where he is now; John answers that Dutch went insane. Reyes says John's story would make a good corrido, a Mexican ballad celebrating heroes, and that more than a hundred have been written about him. He adds that people in the capital already talk about his escape from El Presidio. He then describes Allende as a coward who enjoys beating his people, and as the puppet of Sanchez, a dictator who talks of reform while the people grow poorer.
The target is an army train full of supplies, about to leave the station in Chuparosa. Reyes explains the plan: his men will create a loud distraction while John boards the train and uncouples the army cars. He hands John a knife to kill the guards quietly. John clears the train, takes the engine and brings it to Reyes, who congratulates him and tells him to blow the hatch of the armored car. John cracks the safe inside and keeps its contents. Reyes then rides off to deliver the goods, telling John that his spies will soon have the information he wants. In the meantime, Luisa passes on a request from Reyes in "Father Abraham", and the rebels help John bring down Captain de Santa in "Captain De Santa's Downfall".
At the start of "The Gates of El Presidio", John walks in on Reyes having sex with a woman at Agave Viejo. When John asks what Luisa would say, Reyes first asks who he means. He then advises John to try her himself, and laughs at the idea that she thinks she is his wife. He says he will never marry a peasant: he is going to be the next president of Mexico, and his wife will meet ambassadors and kings. He then announces a present for John: they will bring the man John seeks to justice together.
On the wagon, Reyes has heard of the fight at Sepulcro and of De Santa's death. If John killed De Santa himself, Reyes says he is glad it was his bullet after Chuparosa. If John left him to the rebels, Reyes asks why he did not do it himself. Reyes then reveals that Javier is at El Presidio, the place where they first met, but that he knows nothing of Williamson. He has been told that the two men paid Allende to hide them and have been under his protection since John arrived in Mexico. Taking El Presidio, he says, will open the road to Escalera. When John describes Javier as a cynic who wanted to be a romantic, Reyes answers that John is the opposite.
The ride goes on. Asked who the woman was, Reyes calls her another loyal supporter of a free Mexico and gives no name; John retorts that he cannot even remember the name of the girl he is to marry. He tells John that a man like him cannot belong to one woman, and that he owes it to Mexico to father children and spread noble blood among the peasants. He lists the insults Americans use for Mexicans and calls John a fine socialist when John says he judges a man by how he is treated. Asked about the Chinese workers of the region, Reyes calls them an inferior race. He dreams aloud of storming Allende's mansion in Escalera and sleeping in his silk sheets. Asked what he will do with power once Mexico is his, he says he will give the greatest speech of his life, march on the capital against Sanchez, and delegate. He suggests that John could one day come back to Mexico, like Landon Ricketts, and join his staff.
Near the fort, Reyes explains that his men will launch a feint attack on a side entrance while John drives the wagon, loaded with five crates of TNT, at the main gate and jumps off before it hits. He lights the fuse straight away. The gate blows, the rebels pour in, and Reyes sends John to find Escuella. John catches Javier after a chase; depending on the player, he captures him or kills him. Reyes then calls John back: the army has sent reinforcements. John drives them off with a cannon, and Reyes declares that El Presidio belongs to the rebels. As John comes back for Javier, alive or dead, Reyes comments on the reunion of two old friends. John then hands Javier over to Edgar Ross and Archer Fordham near the border bridge; Reyes is not present.
"An Appointed Time" is the last mission of the second act. The army has executed rebels in Escalera and captured Reyes. John walks through the town as rebels fight the soldiers, and finds Reyes being beaten in the square by Major Raul Zubieta and his men before his execution. Reyes greets John with a mocking welcome to Mexico. John orders the soldiers to let him go and draws his gun. Reyes urges Zubieta to shoot, calling John a drunk. Luisa then rushes at the soldiers with a knife, shouting at them to release her man, and the soldiers guarding Reyes shoot her dead. John kills two of them. Zubieta demands to settle it like men, and John kills him in a duel, then cuts Reyes loose.
Reyes declares that the people are ready and that they will overthrow the colonel that day. A rebel tells him that prisoners in Allende's jail will fight for them, and Reyes sends John to free his men. The rebels then storm the hill. Reyes has John take the machine gun while they blow the gate with gunpowder barrels, then has him defend the gate against soldiers coming up the slope. As the rebels break down the door of the mansion, Allende and Bill Williamson escape in an armored stagecoach. Reyes and John ride after them.
Once the coach is stopped, Allende forces Bill out and offers him to John in exchange for his own life. The player decides: John can shoot Allende, Bill, both or neither, and Reyes shoots the one John spares. In the version given by the wiki's transcript, Reyes kills Allende after a last insult, and Bill is shot by Reyes or John as he reaches for Allende's gun. Reyes then proclaims that Allende is dead and that Mexico is his, and thanks John. John reminds him of the people who died for it, Luisa among them. Reyes cannot remember who she was, promises that a day will be named after her and calls it Laura's Day. Corrected, he says he also knew a Laura.
Reyes invites John to ride with the rebels on the capital. John declines: with Williamson dead, the men who hold his family want him back in Blackwater. Reyes says he will miss him. John doubts Reyes will even remember him, wishes him luck with the revolution and tells him to remember why he wanted power if he wins it. The two men part there, and Reyes does not appear again in the story. The end of the mission opens the West Elizabeth region.
Act III: West Elizabeth
While John hunts Dutch in West Elizabeth, the Blackwater Ledger reports the rest of Reyes's rise. Issue No. 58 announces that Allende, governor of Nuevo Paraíso, "was killed yesterday, following a fire fight with rebels". It adds: "Unsubstantiated reports suggest American mercenaries may have been fighting on both sides during the battle." Reyes calls it a great victory that will lead all the way to the Presidential Palace in Mexico City. He promises to bring greater political freedom to Nuevo Paraíso and to set up a workers council at once. He also releases a new state anthem, "a ballad of his own composition".
Two issues later, in No. 60, the article "Sanchez's Government Falls" reports that Reyes has taken power. After weeks of fighting around the capital, food riots and battles in many provinces, the Fifth Mexican Cavalry Division switched sides after Reyes promised to raise army pay and double leave. His supporters ransacked the president's palace. Sanchez is said to have fled into hiding, though other reports say he was killed. Reyes tells the paper: "Finally, my people, the people of this proud and valiant land are free. A new golden age for our country is just beginning." He promises a period of stability, then elections, and announces a three-day fiesta and a national holiday. Rioting goes on across much of the country, and early reports suggest that up to 100,000 people may have died in the conflict.
The 1914 newspaper
Reyes is still president in 1914. Issue No. 213 of the Blackwater Ledger & Sentinel, which can be read with Jack Marston after John's last mission, carries the article "President Reyes Denies He is a Tyrant, Despite Massacre". It opens on "last month's bloody massacre in a small town south of Mexico City, in which thousands were shot by troops while peacefully protesting the government". The article does not say who gave the order. Reyes denies rumors that he is a tyrant who has gone back on his promise to bring peace to Mexico. He says the path to democratic rule has been slower than he would have liked, but that progress is being made. He points to the new presidential palace as proof that Mexicans can work together, and says plans for a concert hall and other civic buildings remain. He also denies reports of widespread starvation, and says Mexico is about to enter a new golden age.
Undead Nightmare (non-canon)
Undead Nightmare, an expansion released in 2010, is not part of the series' canon. In it, Reyes is the source of the plague. In "Mother Superior Blues", the Mother Superior of Las Hermanas tells John that a woman linked the plague to something Reyes did, and sends him to Escalera, where he must find Reyes in the presidential mansion. In "A Civilized Man", the expansion's final mission, John enters the mansion and finds an undead Reyes chasing a young woman around the dining table. John shouts at him to stop, and Reyes turns on him. He fights like a Bruiser, one of the heavy undead that charge the player, and John puts him down with a shot to the head.
The woman then explains that Reyes took a mask from the catacombs beneath the town, and that putting it back will end the plague. According to the wiki's Undead Nightmare page, she says his lust for invulnerability drove him to steal this ancient Aztec mask. According to the Red Dead Wiki's page on Reyes, he put it on in the belief that it would make him immortal, and it turned him into one of the undead instead. John takes the mask through the catacombs, whose entrance lies in the stands of the bullring behind the mansion, and puts it back on its altar. The woman reveals that she is Ayauhtéotl, an Aztec goddess, and vanishes. John returns to Beecher's Hope to find Abigail and Jack cured. Three months later, in the closing scene "On a Pale Horse", Seth Briars steals the mask from the catacombs, and the dead rise again.
Personality
The Red Dead Wiki quotes the Game of the Year Edition guide of Red Dead Redemption: "He is the 30-year-old son of a wealthy nobleman, who has seemingly sided with the peasants and the rebels to drive out the regime that supports Allende and de Santa. He is very vain and he does not seem to really care about the rights of the poor. However, Mr. Reyes does love to give speeches."
He addresses crowds of rebels from a stump or a town square and ends his speeches with cries of "Viva la revolución!". He calls John my brother, amigo or compadre. He plans to write a poem about his own rescue, says that over a hundred corridos have been written about him, and inflates his own deeds when he retells them. He talks openly about the women he sleeps with and rarely names them. He forgets Luisa Fortuna's name more than once (see Relationships).
In his own words
On his plans, in "The Great Mexican Train Robbery": "When I rule these people, I will be fair and judicious and wise. How so you ask? Very simple... I know these people! I know this land. In short, I can make them better." Asked by John what he will do with power once Mexico is his, in "The Gates of El Presidio": "Like any great leader, my brother, I will delegate." On the Chinese workers, in the same mission: "That is different. They are an inferior race."
On Luisa, when John asks whether he will marry her: "Marry a peasant? My dear boy, don't be absurd. I'm going to be the next president of Mexico. My wife will meet ambassadors, kings, other great men. The very thought that I would marry some peasant girl with the hands of a farmer, well I really don't think so." On his women, a little later: "I owe it to the future of Mexico to breed, my brother. If I can get noble blood flowing through the veins of peasants, can you imagine how great this country can be? An army of Reyes! Of Kings!"
Names
Reyes is the Spanish word for kings, and he plays on it himself (see above). John calls him Abraham or Reyes, and Luisa only ever says Abraham. He presents himself to John as "your brother, Abraham" when he tells him where Javier is hiding. Victor, one of his rebels, cheers "Viva Abraham Reyes!" after the death of Captain de Santa. The Blackwater Ledger calls him Mr. Reyes in 1911 and President Reyes in 1914. According to the Red Dead Wiki, his multiplayer model sometimes speaks of himself in the third person.
Appearance
He is a man of about thirty with straight black hair falling to his shoulders, dark eyes, a mustache and a short black beard on the chin. He usually wears a green poncho over a dark jacket. According to the Red Dead Wiki, his belt buckle bears the arms of the Confederate States of America.
Relationships
John Marston
John frees him from the firing squad at El Presidio, and Reyes's rebels save John from his own execution in Chuparosa (see Biography). On their first ride, Reyes admits he took John for Allende's man and had planned to shoot him in the back. He promises him money, women, even Luisa, then strikes the deal that holds them together: he will help John find his two men, and John will help win his people their freedom. He calls John his brother from then on and receives him at his ranch in Agave Viejo.
John answers most of his speeches with sarcasm, and Reyes tells him sarcasm is beneath a man like him. John threatens him if he is lying about Williamson and Escuella; Reyes asks for time. Reyes says John's story would make a fine corrido, calls him a romantic who wants to be a cynic, and offers him a place on his staff if he ever returns to Mexico. After Escalera, John declines to ride on the capital and tells him to remember why he wanted power (see Biography). In Undead Nightmare, which is not canon, John kills the undead Reyes (see Biography).
Luisa Fortuna
Luisa believes she is to marry him after the revolution and tells John that his family does not approve of a peasant girl. She sends John to free him at El Presidio and relays his orders in her own missions (see Biography). Reyes calls her Laura from their first scene, laughs at the idea of marrying her and recommends her to John (see In his own words and Quotes). She is shot dead in Escalera trying to save him (see Biography). Afterwards, he has to ask who she was and proposes a day in her honor under the wrong name; he then explains that he once knew a girl called Laura.
Agustin Allende
The governor of Nuevo Paraíso and the target of his revolt. Reyes describes him to John as a puppet of President Sanchez, a lapdog who beats the people he swore to protect and enjoys it. He looks forward to sleeping in Allende's silk sheets at Escalera. Allende, for his part, tells John that men like Javier's father are natural allies for Reyes. Allende dies on the road out of Escalera, shot by John or by Reyes (see Biography).
Vincente de Santa
The army captain hunts Reyes and has nothing but contempt for him: to John he calls him a traitor, a coward and a liar, and says Reyes writes poems about battles he never fought. De Santa betrays John in Chuparosa just before Reyes arrives (see Biography). When De Santa dies at El Sepulcro, Reyes congratulates John if he fired the shot, or asks why he left it to the rebels.
Javier Escuella
Reyes's spies locate Javier at El Presidio, where, according to Reyes, he and Bill paid Allende for protection. Reyes has heard that Javier comes from the province, was once a bounty hunter and fought among the early revolutionaries (see Biography). The rebels' assault on the fort lets John reach him (see Biography). When John brings Javier out, alive or dead, Reyes comments on the reunion of two old friends.
Bill Williamson
Reyes cannot tell John whether Bill is at El Presidio with Javier. Bill flees Escalera in Allende's stagecoach and is shot by John or, if the player does not fire, by Reyes (see Biography).
Dutch van der Linde
The two men never meet. On the road to Chuparosa, John describes his old leader as a man who robbed the rich and believed change had to be brutal; Reyes asks whether Dutch was a revolutionary like him, approves of another violent idealist and asks where he is now.
Landon Ricketts
The two men share no scene. Ricketts tells John that Luisa is close to the rebel leader. Reyes cites Ricketts as a famous outlaw who came to Mexico to die in the sunshine, and suggests John might do the same.
Ignacio Sanchez
The president of Mexico, never seen in the game. Reyes calls him the country's kind and generous dictator, with heavy irony, and holds him responsible for everything Allende does. He plans to march on the capital once Escalera falls; the newspapers report the outcome (see Biography).
His family
According to the Blackwater Ledger, he is a nobleman's son born on a ranch outside the capital (see Biography). He says his late mother would turn in her grave if he married a peasant. Luisa tells John that his family disapproves of her. The Red Dead Wiki lists an unnamed father and an unnamed mother, dead.
His rebels
Reyes' Rebels, named after him, are based at Agave Viejo. Victor tells John that women and even children fight in their ranks. In "Father Abraham", Luisa passes on Reyes's requests: ambush Allende's reinforcements, destroy the army's supplies and kill everyone, because Abraham wants to send a message. The prisoners John frees in Escalera declare themselves loyal to him (see Biography).
Raul Zubieta
The army major who holds Reyes in Escalera. Tied up and beaten, Reyes jokes that they are discussing the future of the country, then tells Zubieta that the American is a drunk and that he would pull the trigger in his place. John kills him in a duel (see Biography).
Espinoza
The army captain tells John that Reyes tries hard to recruit Americans, with propaganda everywhere promising women and gold. John kills him in Chuparosa (see Biography).
Herbert Moon
The Armadillo shopkeeper speaks of him in two lines quoted by the Red Dead Wiki: "Reyes is as revolutionary as Napoleon! He just likes riding horses in a fancy uniform." and "Sanchez, Allende, De Santa, Reyes; all the same! They just want Armadillo rail rides."
Ayauhtéotl
Undead Nightmare (non-canon): the woman the undead Reyes chases round the table of the Escalera mansion; she tells John that Reyes took a mask from the catacombs (see Biography).
Propaganda, weapons and horse
Speeches, songs and posters
Reyes recruits through words. He gives speeches after each victory (see Biography) and says that songs reach the people better than newspapers in a country where most cannot read. He counts the corridos written in his honor. His face is on posters across the map: according to the Red Dead Wiki, one hangs at Casa Madrugada, three in Escalera, one in Tesoro Azul, one at Solomon's Folly, one at Benedict Point and one on the side of the assay office in Tumbleweed. John tells him he has seen them.
In the game
Reyes gives three story missions in the second act of Red Dead Redemption, two of them starting at his base at Agave Viejo, and appears in two others; the Missions table lists them. Each fails if John attacks or kills him, and, in "The Great Mexican Train Robbery" and "An Appointed Time", if John kills his horse. According to the mission pages, he does not fight in "Must a Savior Die?" or "The Great Mexican Train Robbery": in the second, he draws the guards' attention while John boards the train, after handing him a knife. In "An Appointed Time", the mission page notes that he keeps firing at the stagecoach if John ties up its drivers instead of killing them, and that he shoots both Bill and Allende if John lassoes Bill.
Once "An Appointed Time" is complete, his rebels replace the army as the law in Nuevo Paraíso, according to the Red Dead Wiki, and the last piece of the Reyes' Rebels Outfit goes on sale in Escalera. Its in-game description reads: "Disguise yourself as one of Reyes' Rebels and you will be treated kindly by the law in Mexico." Undead Nightmare (non-canon): the undead Reyes is a Bruiser, an undead that charges at the player, and the Red Dead Wiki calls him the slimmest of them; it adds that he seems to be the only undead with more than one set of sounds. In "A Civilized Man", John picks up a Semi-auto Shotgun in the catacombs and remarks that Reyes brought protection.
Weapons and horse
The Red Dead Wiki lists a Hunting Knife, a Schofield Revolver, a Winchester Repeater and a Rolling Block Rifle among his weapons, plus the Semi-auto Shotgun of Undead Nightmare. According to the wiki, the Winchester Repeater is his main weapon: he uses it at El Presidio and in Escalera, and, depending on the player, to kill Bill Williamson or Colonel Allende. The wiki gives him a Cleveland Bay as his mount.
Missions
Reyes appears in five story missions of Red Dead Redemption, all in the second act, set in Nuevo Paraíso. He gives three of them himself, from his base at Agave Viejo or in Escalera. The first two belong to the strands of Luisa Fortuna and Vincente de Santa. "Cowards Die Many Times" unlocks his own strand, and "An Appointed Time", the last mission of the act, opens West Elizabeth and the strand of Edgar Ross. In Undead Nightmare, he is the target of the final mission.

Development and performance
Casting
Abraham Reyes appears in Red Dead Redemption and its expansion Undead Nightmare, both released in 2010. Wikipedia credits Josh Segarra with the role, in its article on the game and in his own article. Segarra is an American actor of Puerto Rican descent, born on June 3, 1986 in Longwood, Florida, and a graduate of New York University's Tisch School of the Arts. At the time of Red Dead Redemption he played Hector Ruiz in the PBS children's series The Electric Company (2009 to 2011). He later created the role of Emilio Estefan in the Broadway musical On Your Feet!, which opened on November 5, 2015, played Adrian Chase in the fifth season of Arrow (2016), and appeared in She-Hulk: Attorney at Law (2022) and Scream VI (2023). No interview in which he discusses the role was found, and no source consulted names a performer for Reyes's motion capture.
Before release
According to the Red Dead Wiki, Reyes looks neater in the screenshots published before the game's release, with shorter hair and a shorter goatee. The wiki's page on the game's cut content adds that an unused bounty poster of Reyes was left in the game files, with a goatee model that is not used in the final game.
Reception
Reviews
Game Informer's review of Red Dead Redemption, written by Matt Bertz and published on May 17, 2010, gave the game 9.75 out of 10. Bertz does not name Reyes, but describes the Mexican part of the story as John having to "get cozy with both the corrupt ruler of the border region and the upstart rebel hell-bent on taking power for himself." He judged that this "tangential plot drags on far too long without engaging the task at hand, and the game's momentum suffers for it."
Character lists
In a list of the ten best characters of the Red Dead Redemption franchise, published by Sportskeeda on August 13, 2023, Aniket Mukherjee ranked Reyes tenth, just behind Irish (ninth). He wrote that Reyes "is ranked 10th because of his significant contribution to the Mexican revolution, which adds depth to the Red Dead Redemption's narrative by introducing political intrigue."
Academic study
In "The Mustang Diaries: A Study of Mexico in Red Dead Redemption 1", published in the Revista Panamericana de Comunicación (vol. 4, no. 2, 2022, online in April 2023), Javier Aldape, a doctoral student in Hispanic studies at Texas A&M University, reads Allende and Reyes as the two sides of the caudillo figure. He calls Reyes "The revolutionary Messiah who will restore peace to Mexico" and writes: "He womanizes and makes the same promise to all of them, which he never keeps." He also notes that Reyes is "more concerned about corridos of him than the heroic deeds of the soldaderas", the women who fought in the revolution.
Other media and legacy
Multiplayer
Reyes became a playable character model in the multiplayer mode of Red Dead Redemption with the Liars and Cheats Pack, detailed by Rockstar on August 27, 2010 and released on September 21, 2010. Players select him in the Damnation section of the Outfitter, with Agustin Allende, Bill Williamson, Dutch van der Linde, Edgar Ross, Harold MacDougal and Irish. According to the Red Dead Wiki, he sometimes speaks of himself in the third person in multiplayer, and many of his lines are in Spanish (see Quotes). The versions of Red Dead Redemption released on PlayStation 4 and Nintendo Switch on August 17, 2023, then on PC on October 29, 2024, contain the story and Undead Nightmare but not the multiplayer mode, so the Reyes model is not available in them.
Marketing
Rockstar released official artwork of Reyes for Red Dead Redemption, and pre-release screenshots that show him, one of them with John. According to the Red Dead Wiki, his goatee and hair are slightly shorter in these screenshots than in the final game. Reyes does not appear in Red Dead Redemption 2 or in Red Dead Online, which are set before 1911; the Red Dead Wiki lists him only in Red Dead Redemption and Undead Nightmare.
Trivia
Red Dead Redemption
According to the Red Dead Wiki, the opening of "The Gates of El Presidio" is the only sex scene shown on screen in Red Dead Redemption. According to the mission page, the Game of the Year Edition covered the woman's breasts with clothing, a change reverted in later versions of the game.
When "The Gates of El Presidio" is replayed, Reyes always speaks as if the rebels, not John, had killed De Santa, according to the mission page.
According to the mission pages, "Must a Savior Die?" and "The Great Mexican Train Robbery" are the only two missions in which Reyes does not fight. In the first, hitting Reyes with the horse while picking him up fails the mission for being too aggressive.
According to the mission page of "The Great Mexican Train Robbery", if John fires his gun inside the armored car, Reyes asks him whether he is shooting at imaginary soldiers.
According to the mission page of "An Appointed Time", Reyes keeps firing at the stagecoach if John ties up its drivers instead of killing them. If John lassoes Bill while Allende holds him, Reyes shoots both men, which can block the mission. In rare cases, when John shoots Bill, Allende begs, runs into the brush and is shot in the back by Reyes outside of any cutscene.
After "An Appointed Time", Reyes's rebels replace the army as the armed force of Escalera and most of Nuevo Paraíso, and the last scrap of the Reyes' Rebels outfit goes on sale at the Escalera general store.
According to the Red Dead Wiki, posters of Reyes can be found in Casa Madrugada (in the alley leading to the shooting gallery), three in Escalera, one in Tesoro Azul, and, north of the border, at Solomon's Folly, at Benedict Point and on the assay office in Tumbleweed.
According to the Red Dead Wiki, Drew MacFarlane sometimes wears the same outfit as Reyes in "Must a Savior Die?" and "An Appointed Time".
Undead Nightmare (non-canon)
According to the Red Dead Wiki, the undead Reyes is the thinnest of all the Bruisers in the game. He is also the only undead with two sets of sounds: he groans like the ordinary undead while idle, and roars like the Bruisers when he charges.
According to the mission page of "A Civilized Man", the undead Reyes's body cannot be searched: after the cutscene that follows his death it stays inside the mansion, whose doors are locked until the mission is over. In the catacombs, John picks up a Semi-auto Shotgun and remarks that Reyes brought protection.
