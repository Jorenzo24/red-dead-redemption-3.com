
Irish is an Irish arms dealer and heavy drinker in Red Dead Redemption (2010), set in 1911. Nigel West Dickens sends John Marston to him for the firepower needed against Fort Mercer, the hideout of Bill Williamson's gang. John saves him from two former friends who are drowning him in Armadillo. Irish first sends John to an empty cabin, then leads him to the Gatling gun hidden in a mine at Gaptooth Breach, and later to the ammunition held by his contact Shaky in Thieves' Landing. He promises to fight at Fort Mercer but arrives only once the fight is over.
He then takes John across the river to Mexico on his raft, under fire from gunmen on the Mexican bank, where he admits there is a bounty on his head, and leaves him on the other bank. A 1914 newspaper notice, which the Red Dead Wiki attributes to him, reports the accidental death of an Irishman of unknown name in Thieves' Landing. He is voiced by K. Harrison Sweeney.
Key facts
In "A Frenchman, a Welshman and an Irishman", John saves him from Welsh and French in Armadillo; Irish then sends him to a cabin at Lake Don Julio where the gun is not.
In "Man is Born Unto Trouble", he leads John to the Gatling gun in a mine at Gaptooth Breach and works the lift that brings it up.
In "On Shaky's Ground", John catches him robbing two nuns in Thieves' Landing; Irish then takes him to Shaky and drives the ammunition to MacFarlane's Ranch.
He misses "The Assault on Fort Mercer" and turns up only at the end.
In "We Shall Be Together in Paradise", he takes John across the river to Mexico by raft; it is his last appearance.

Biography
Origins
Irish is an Irish immigrant who deals in stolen goods and weapons in New Austin. The game never gives his real name. Everything known about his past comes from what he tells John Marston in 1911. He says he met the two men he calls French and Welsh on the boat over to America a few years earlier, and that the three had been as close as thieves ever since. His father used to warn him never to trust a Welshman; Irish adds that his father had his throat slit, without saying by whom. He swears on his poor mother's life, which the Red Dead Wiki reads as a sign that she is still alive in 1911. He calls himself a good Catholic. He tells John that he has been to Mexico before, that he was very drunk the whole time, and that he has many friends there.
The Red Dead Wiki adds details that do not appear in the mission transcripts. According to the wiki, Irish says he grew up poor and hungry and left Ireland for the United States at the age of fourteen, shortly after he was caught in bed with his brother's wife; the wiki places that remark in "Man is Born Unto Trouble". The same source says he claims to have killed at least one man, whom he calls Twinkle-Toe Dave. According to the wiki's list of his lines, one of the optional conversations on the ride to the lake has him say that he was the brains of the trio and that the other two had become unreliable.
Red Dead Redemption (1911)
In 1911, agents of the Bureau of Investigation hold John Marston's wife and son to force him to hunt down Bill Williamson, who holds Fort Mercer in New Austin. To take the fort, John works with the swindler Nigel West Dickens, who plans to drive him inside hidden in his wagon. Irish appears in five missions of Red Dead Redemption, all at the end of the first act: the three missions of his own strand, "The Assault on Fort Mercer" and "We Shall Be Together in Paradise", which takes John into Mexico. He does not appear after that.
Act I: New Austin
In "Liars, Cheats and Other Proud Americans", Dickens tells John that the plan now needs heavy firepower and sends him to an old friend of his who goes by the name of Irish. He says Irish can usually be found in Armadillo or a nearby town, in the middle of some drinking spree. John concludes that he is dealing with an alcoholic arms dealer.
In "A Frenchman, a Welshman and an Irishman", John walks into a barn in Armadillo where Welsh and French are holding Irish's head under the water of a trough. French says Irish stole their gun and tried to steal their horses. Irish denies everything and blames French, who, he says, is manipulating Welsh. John tells the two men to let him go. They turn on John, and he kills them both. John then explains that Dickens said Irish could help him find a machine gun, and reminds him that he has just saved his life. Irish thanks him, describes the gun as magnificent government issue and leads the way, while saying the saloon is waiting for him. Issue No. 56 of the Blackwater Ledger, on sale later in the story, carries two notices about the men: Alwyn Lloyd, "believed to be of Welsh origin", died by gunshot in Armadillo, and Leander Holland, "known to friends by the name of French", was found dead in the same town.
On the way, Irish calls the two dead men his only friends in the world and says he is glad to see them dead. When John doubts his story, Irish tells him he never asked for help and owes him nothing; John answers that he will decide what Irish owes him. Irish leads him to a ridge above a cabin on Lake Don Julio, where he says the thieves keep the gun inside a shack. Asked to help, he offers to cover John from the ridge, saying he is better at long range. The mission page says he disappears as soon as he sees the guards. John kills the armed men at the cabin alone, goes inside and finds no machine gun, and calls Irish a liar.
At the start of "Man is Born Unto Trouble", John finds Irish passed out against a building in Benedict Point, talking in his sleep to a young lady about her corset. John kicks him awake. Irish says he has found a gun and does not remember being saved; John threatens him if no Gatling gun turns up within the hour. Irish blames the whiskey for his memory. On the road, he swears his intentions were pure and calls the cabin an honest mistake. He then says some miners have been boasting of a machine gun they found and hidden at Gaptooth Breach. John says that Irish and Dickens are as crooked as each other. Irish adds that a wagon will be needed, as the gun is very heavy, which makes John ask how he was supposed to carry it alone the first time.
Outside the mining camp, Irish explains that a lift is used to bring heavy ore out of a shaft, and that John can use it to bring the gun to the surface. He says he would go himself but the mine plays havoc with his sinuses, and leaves to hide the horses and come back with a borrowed flat wagon. John fights his way through the camp and the mine, finds the gun in a mine cart and pushes it to the lift. Irish works the gears and brings John and the gun up, then calls it God's own gun. John rides the cart down the hill to the wagon Irish has brought, and Irish talks of delivering the weapon to Dickens. According to the mission page, Irish then leaves with the wagon to take the gun to him. Issue No. 56 of the Blackwater Ledger later reports gunfire and explosions at the Gaptooth Breach mines, without naming anyone.
The gun cannot be used yet. In Seth Briars's mission "A Gentle Drive with Friends", if John already has the machine gun, Seth passes on a message from Dickens: it has no ammunition. John answers that he will have to pay Irish another visit. In "The Sport of Kings, and Liars", Dickens says the team will be ready once Seth and Irish have delivered their part, and John sums up the team as a con man, a grave robber and a drunk.
At the start of "On Shaky's Ground", John finds Irish in an alley of Thieves' Landing, holding up two nuns at gunpoint. Irish turns his revolver on John and asks who he is. John takes the gun from him, strikes him on the head with its grip and warns him never again to pretend he has forgotten his name or his debt. Irish says he took the nuns for prostitutes. John apologizes to the two women and lets them go. When John says the Gatling gun does not work, Irish claims he was on his way to see him when the drink got the better of him, and says he knows where to find what is missing. Walking through town, John calls him a coward who robs nuns; Irish answers that John has robbed as many innocent people as he has, and that the church has more money than anyone.
Irish takes John to a warehouse in Thieves' Landing, where a contact of his, a stammering man called Shaky, has arranged to supply the ammunition. The front gate is locked although it was meant to be open, so Irish leads John up to a back door. Through it, they hear Shaky being beaten by men who want to know who he works with. Irish sends John in to free him and leaves to fetch a wagon. John frees Shaky, and the two fight their way out to the yard and the ammunition. Irish arrives with the wagon once the shooting has stopped. When Shaky says they are even, Irish answers that Shaky still owes him for morphine pills meant to calm his nerves, and mocks his stammer.
Irish drives the wagon out of Thieves' Landing while John rides beside him and shoots the riders who chase them, as well as a gunman on a bridge. John notes that Irish always misses the action; Irish replies that someone has to drive. Near MacFarlane's Ranch, Irish proposes a permanent partnership, which John turns down, saying he has had enough of liars. John then tells him that he will be at his side when he attacks the fort, and that it is time he pulled the trigger for once. Irish agrees. At the ranch, he leaves, saying that Dickens's wagon will soon be rigged and ready.
"The Assault on Fort Mercer" brings together John, Marshal Leigh Johnson, his deputies Eli and Jonah, Dickens and Seth. Irish supplied the Gatling gun but misses the fight. Dickens drives the wagon into the fort with John hidden in the back, at the gun, and the posse clears the fort and drives off the gang's reinforcements. Irish only turns up once it is over, saying he missed it. Bill is not in the fort: Jonah reports that a captured gang member says Bill left for Mexico the morning before, somewhere near Chuparosa. Irish then offers to take John there, saying he is very popular down there and has many friends in the south. He tells John to meet him at the ferry and goes off to collect his things.
In "We Shall Be Together in Paradise", the mission that takes him across the border, John meets Irish at Brittlebrush Trawl, where Dickens has come to wish him well. Irish says that Mexico is like a second home to him and that he has more friends there than he can count. His plan is to board his raft and let the current carry them to paradise. He asks John whether he has ever done him wrong; John replies that it was not for lack of trying. Dickens says goodbye, and the raft sets off down the San Luis River without him. On the water, John reproaches Irish for missing the fight at Fort Mercer. Irish explains that he woke up in a woman's company and felt it would be rude to leave, and says he will be late to his own funeral.
Armed men open fire from the Mexican bank. One of them shouts in Spanish that Irish is a traitor and that he will kill him. Irish admits that he should have mentioned there is a bounty or two on his head in Mexico, which he links to money and possibly to a misunderstanding about a man's daughter, though he no longer remembers the details. He declares that he came into the world fighting and will leave it fighting; John expects him to leave it wetting himself. Between two attacks, Irish praises the Mexican women and pulque, and claims that people there call him El Rato (see Names). The attacks go on from the rocks and the clifftops, and Irish calls out where the shooters are. According to the Red Dead Wiki, he admits during the crossing that he cannot swim.
The raft lands on a beach where horses are waiting. John asks where to start looking. Irish says he was very drunk on his last visit. He remembers an American he saw shoot a man and with whom he drank in Chuparosa, then wonders whether that was in Canada. He also suggests the provincial governor, a colonel based in Escalera whose Spanish name he cannot remember, and says he once played cards with him. The game names neither man; the Red Dead Wiki links the first to Landon Ricketts and the second to Agustin Allende. Irish then says the local hospitality is not what it was and that he never overstays his welcome. He wishes John luck, tells him he is not a bad man despite his temper and his looks, and leaves. It is his last appearance in Red Dead Redemption.
The 1914 newspaper notice
Act III: West Elizabeth
Issue No. 213 of the Blackwater Ledger & Sentinel, dated 1914, becomes available after John's last mission, "The Last Enemy That Shall Be Destroyed", with Jack Marston as the playable character. Among the short notices of its Miscellany column is this one: "Irish male, of unknown name, came to his death by the accidental discharge of a gun in a Thieves' Landing outhouse." The notice gives no date and no name. The Red Dead Wiki attributes it to Irish and lists him as dead in 1914.
Undead Nightmare (non-canon)
Undead Nightmare, an expansion released in 2010, is not part of the series' canon. Irish does not appear in it, which makes him one of the few main characters of Red Dead Redemption absent from the expansion. According to the Red Dead Wiki, a Retcher, one of the expansion's undead types, can sometimes be met that looks very much like him. The wiki also describes a half-eaten corpse lying next to the Armadillo train station that resembles him, with a Repeater Carbine beside the body.
Personality
The Red Dead Wiki quotes a description of the character by Rockstar: "A teller of tall tales, who'll try to talk his way out of anything." Wikipedia's summary of Red Dead Redemption calls him an alcoholic arms smuggler. In his missions, John finds him drunk or passed out more often than sober, and he blames his poor memory on whiskey. He forgets John's name, or pretends to, between two missions.
He avoids danger whenever he can. He offers to cover John from a ridge, says the mines play havoc with his sinuses, and volunteers to fetch the wagon while John goes in. In the only fight he takes part in, on the river, he shoots back and calls out where the attackers are. He boasts of contacts and friends who turn out to be hostile, and admits his memories of Mexico are blurred by drink. His speech is full of Irish turns of phrase (me for my, feck, shite) and of colorful comparisons. He calls John Johnny, Johnny boy, fella, dovey or smiler.
In his own words
Woken up in Benedict Point, in "Man is Born Unto Trouble", he explains his lapses: "It's the whiskey, sir... It gives me the memory of a new born babe. As innocent as can be." On the two men who were drowning him, in "A Frenchman, a Welshman and an Irishman": "Life's too short for regrets... and I'd outgrown them fools." On the wagon out of Thieves' Landing, in "On Shaky's Ground", when John points out that he missed the shooting: "Someone's gotta drive the wagon, don't they? Team work, Johnny boy. That's my game. Not just the glory, like you."
On the raft, in "We Shall Be Together in Paradise": "You know me, Johnny boy. I'll be late to me own funeral. They said God invented whiskey to stop the Irish from rulin' the world." Under fire a few minutes later: "I came into this world fightin'. And I'll go out of it fightin'!"
Names
Irish is a nickname, and the game never gives him another name. Wikipedia lists him as Irish, and Nigel West Dickens calls him Mr. Irish. When he sings to himself in Benedict Point, he calls himself Uncle Irish. Welsh and French insult him as a paddy and a mick. On the raft he claims that in Mexico he is known as El Rato: "Down here, they call me El Rato. The cat. On account of me stealth and cunning." John corrects him: "I'm pretty sure Rato means "rat," my friend." John adds that he likes it, as it is a little more inventive than Irish. The 1914 newspaper notice that the Red Dead Wiki attributes to him describes an Irish man of unknown name (see Biography). According to the French-language Red Dead Wiki, the French version of the game calls him l'Irlandais.
Appearance
He has long, shaggy dark hair and a thick dark beard. He wears a worn, pale shirt with rolled-up sleeves over his trousers. He carries his revolver tucked into the front of his trousers rather than in a holster, as the Red Dead Wiki and the mission page of "We Shall Be Together in Paradise" note.
Relationships
John Marston
John meets him by saving his life in an Armadillo barn and holds that debt over him in every mission that follows (see Biography). John's patience runs out quickly: he threatens Irish several times, calls him a liar, a coward and a lazy drunk, and refuses his offer of a permanent partnership because he has had enough of liars. Irish answers that John would get more help if he were more cordial with people.
After the nuns at Thieves' Landing, Irish calls John a hypocrite who has robbed as many innocent people as he has; John answers that he tried to rob only those who had more than they deserved (see Quotes). John makes Irish promise to fight at Fort Mercer, and Irish does not turn up until the fight is over (see Biography). When they part on the Mexican bank, Irish tells John that he is an angry and ugly man, but not a bad one (see Quotes).
Nigel West Dickens
Dickens calls Irish an old friend and sends John to find him, usually in Armadillo or a nearby town on a drinking spree; John sums him up as an alcoholic arms dealer. On their first ride, Irish calls Dickens a swindling charlatan who talks endlessly, and says one dose of his tonic once upset his stomach for weeks (see Quotes). The Gatling gun Irish finds is delivered to Dickens and mounted in his wagon (see Biography). At Brittlebrush Trawl, Dickens chats with him before the crossing, calls him an interesting fellow and says he is well connected south of the border; Irish laughs when Dickens claims that men like him are revered abroad.
Shaky
A stuttering smuggler who works out of the Thieves' Landing warehouse, and Irish's source for the Gatling gun ammunition. Irish leaves John to free him from the men beating him while he goes for the wagon, calling Shaky a good man (see Biography). When Shaky says they are even, Irish mocks his stutter and claims Shaky still owes him for morphine pills taken to calm his nerves; Shaky swears at him (see Quotes). The mission fails if Shaky is assaulted or killed.
Welsh (Alwyn Lloyd) and French
Irish says he met these two men on the boat to America and that the three of them have been as thick as thieves ever since (see Origins). In 1911 they are drowning him in a horse trough in Armadillo, accusing him of stealing their gun and trying to steal their horses; Irish blames French, and John kills both (see Biography). Irish then says they were his only friends in the world and that he is glad to see them dead. According to the Red Dead Wiki, a notice in the Blackwater Ledger names Welsh as Alwyn Lloyd, believed to be of Welsh origin and killed by gunshot in Armadillo; the wiki gives French's name as Leander Holland. After the fight, John remarks that French did not sound very French.
Seth Briars
Both are recruited through Dickens for the Fort Mercer plan, but they share no scene. When Seth passes on Dickens's message that the machine gun needs ammunition, John says he will have to pay Irish another visit, which leads to "On Shaky's Ground" (see Biography).
Bill Williamson
Irish never meets him. He helps arm the assault on Bill's hideout, then offers to guide John to Mexico when the prisoner at Fort Mercer says Bill has gone near Chuparosa, promising they will find him without trouble (see Biography).
Landon Ricketts
On the Mexican bank, Irish tells John about an American he once saw shoot a man and drank with in Chuparosa, before wondering whether that was in Canada (see Biography). The game does not name this man; the Red Dead Wiki identifies him as Landon Ricketts, whom John meets on arriving in Chuparosa.
Agustin Allende
Irish suggests the provincial governor as another lead: a colonel based in Escalera, whose Spanish name he cannot recall and with whom he says he played cards (see Biography). He cannot remember whether the man was pleasant or a bastard. The Red Dead Wiki identifies this colonel as Agustin Allende.
The Mexican gunmen
Armed men on the Mexican bank attack the raft, one of them calling Irish a traitor (see Biography). Irish admits there is a bounty or two on his head in Mexico, linked, as far as he remembers, to money and to a misunderstanding about a man's daughter. He concludes that it is the women of Mexico, not the men, who love him.
His family
The game shows none of his relatives. Irish says his father warned him never to trust a Welshman and had his throat slit, and he swears on his mother's life that his intentions were pure. For his brother and the reasons he left home, see Origins.
Contraband, weapons and horses
Contraband and contacts
Nigel West Dickens presents him as the man who can supply the firepower for Fort Mercer. Irish says he knows guns front, back and sideways, and the Red Dead Wiki lists his occupation as contraband dealer. His Thieves' Landing contact, Shaky, provides the ammunition for the Gatling gun (see Relationships). He claims to have friends across the border, and says he has been to Mexico and to Canada (see Biography).
Weapons
The Red Dead Wiki lists a Cattleman Revolver and a Repeater Carbine. According to the mission page, "We Shall Be Together in Paradise" is the only mission in which he fights, with his Cattleman Revolver. In "On Shaky's Ground" he draws a revolver on two nuns and then on John, who takes it from him (see Biography). John mocks him for never using it, saying cobwebs must be growing on his holster.
Horses, wagons and the raft
He rides his own horse with John in New Austin. At Gaptooth Breach he works the lift that brings John and the Gatling gun up from the mine, then waits below with a borrowed flatbed wagon. In "On Shaky's Ground" he drives the wagon loaded with ammunition while John rides shotgun. He crosses the San Luis River with John on his own raft. According to the Red Dead Wiki, he admits during the crossing that he cannot swim. On the Mexican bank, he looks over the horses waiting there before he and John ride off.
In the game
Irish appears in five missions: the three of his own strand in New Austin, "The Assault on Fort Mercer" and the first mission in Mexico; the Missions table lists them. Each of them fails if John assaults or kills Irish. The four New Austin missions also fail if John abandons him or kills the horse he rides, and the river crossing fails if John abandons him or shoots the rope of the raft. According to the Red Dead Wiki, if John attacks him before one of his missions, Irish shoots back or runs away and cannot be killed. Right after "We Shall Be Together in Paradise", he can be killed only if the player acts very quickly, usually with Dead Eye; otherwise he rides off and is not seen again.
Missions
Irish has a strand of three story missions in the first act of Red Dead Redemption, numbered 19 to 21 in the story order. The first, "A Frenchman, a Welshman and an Irishman", becomes available after Nigel West Dickens's "Liars, Cheats and Other Proud Americans". His strand is one of the four that must be completed before Leigh Johnson's "The Assault on Fort Mercer", with those of Johnson, Dickens and Seth Briars. He also gives "We Shall Be Together in Paradise", the 25th mission, which opens Nuevo Paraíso and the second act. He has no mission in Undead Nightmare.

Development and performance
Casting
Wikipedia credits K. Harrison Sweeney as the voice of Irish in Red Dead Redemption (2010). Sweeney described his casting in a written interview published on a Red Dead Wiki user blog on November 10, 2011. According to him, his agent put him forward for an interactive game set in the Old West, and his audition called for an Irishman. The casting associates warned the actors in the waiting room that the producers were British, and that anyone not from Ireland or Wales should not bother. Sweeney says he can switch between five Irish dialects. The actors were asked to walk across the room so the casting team could see how they moved, then to improvise dialogue in front of a camera.
Sweeney says he grew up in Wyoming and that his ancestry is mostly German and Scandinavian. He recalls that the director, Rod Edge, a Briton, and the producers were taken aback when they learned he was American.
Recording
In the same interview, Sweeney says the actors only received the lines of their own scenes. He shot for a few days in January 2009, came back for more missions in June 2009, and recorded the ambient lines, such as walking around or riding drunk, in a voice-over booth in October 2009; the last session took place in New York. He names some of his improvisations that were kept: Irish checking the horses, his mockery of Shaky's stutter and the exclamation that follows it. According to Sweeney, Edge reworked the oath Irish mutters after John knocks him down in front of the nuns.
Sweeney calls the role his first opportunity to do motion capture and thanks Rockstar Games for it. He adds that the producers, having heard him play with voices between takes, had him record a few one-line parts for characters they had not cast, including a Mexican colonel he does not name.
Absence from Undead Nightmare
Irish does not appear in Undead Nightmare, the 2010 expansion of Red Dead Redemption. Asked why in the 2011 interview, Sweeney first answers with a joke, then explains that he had booked another job while Rockstar was shooting the expansion, and says he would have loved to be in it.
After the game
On January 27, 2012, Kotaku (Kirk Hamilton) relayed an announcement by Steve J. Palmer, the voice of Bill Williamson: a redneck zombie romantic comedy titled From the Trailer to the Grave, written by Sweeney, was to be shot in Wyoming in the fall of 2012 with five actors from Red Dead Redemption, among them Sweeney, Rob Wiethoff (John Marston), Brad Carter (Jonah) and Anthony De Longis (Leigh Johnson). In his 2011 interview, Sweeney already said he had written parts in the film for several members of the cast.
Reception
Before and at launch
On March 25, 2010, Game Informer (Matt Bertz) described a new Rockstar teaser presenting the morally questionable inhabitants of the game's world, listing "a drunken, gun-wielding Irishman" among them and comparing the cast to that of the HBO series Deadwood. On May 20, 2010, the day before the game's release in Ireland and Britain, the Dublin newspaper Herald.ie published an article by Cormac Byrne titled "Irish 'drunk' sours launch of hit game". It warned that "the stereotype of the drunken Paddy" would again be exploited, since the game's "town drunk" was called Irish, and quoted a description of the character taken from a gaming forum.
The actor's response
Asked about the criticism in the November 2011 interview, Sweeney answered that people worried about the stereotype should relax: "It's FICTION, people." He went on to advise critics, in a joking tone, to relax and play the game.
Character lists
In a list of the ten best characters of the Red Dead Redemption franchise published by Sportskeeda (page last modified on August 13, 2023), Aniket Mukherjee ranked Irish ninth, just behind Seth Briars, and called him "the rowdy and always tipsy arms dealer".
Other media and legacy
Multiplayer
Irish became a playable character model in the multiplayer mode of Red Dead Redemption with the Liars and Cheats Pack, detailed by Rockstar on August 27, 2010 and released on September 21, 2010. Players select him in the Damnation section of the Outfitter, with Abraham Reyes, Agustin Allende, Bill Williamson, Dutch van der Linde, Edgar Ross and Harold MacDougal. According to the Red Dead Wiki, his multiplayer model carries its revolver in a holster on the hip, and the wiki lists 29 taunts for him, a few of which are quoted above. The versions of Red Dead Redemption released on PlayStation 4 and Nintendo Switch on August 17, 2023, then on PC on October 29, 2024, do not include the multiplayer mode.
Red Dead Redemption 2 and Red Dead Online
Irish does not appear in Red Dead Redemption 2 or Red Dead Online. His contact Shaky does: according to the Red Dead Wiki, Shaky is a stranger in Red Dead Online, set in 1898, where he gives the player paid jobs in Thieves' Landing.
Trivia
The title "A Frenchman, a Welshman and an Irishman" plays on the European jokes that start with an Englishman, an Irishman and a Scotsman, whose nationalities change with the country telling them. The title "Man is Born Unto Trouble" comes from the Book of Job (5:7): "Yet man is born unto trouble, as the sparks fly upward." According to its mission page, "We Shall Be Together in Paradise" refers both to the promise of paradise made to one of the two criminals crucified with Jesus and to Nuevo Paraíso, where the mission leads.
On the way to the Gaptooth Breach mine, Irish sings "I love me faithless Flora, the Lily of the West" (transcript wording; the character page gives "precious Flora"). "Lily of the West" is a traditional ballad. According to the mission page, it comes from the west of Ireland, where the woman is called Flora, while the version popular in the United States names her Mary.
The page of "The Assault on Fort Mercer" lists Irish among the posse in its summary, and its trivia notes that he can be seen next to John in the opening cutscene, although he takes no part in the fight.
According to the page of "On Shaky's Ground", the order of the missions allows a mismatch: Seth can still pass on the message that the gun has no ammunition after John has already fetched it with Irish.
According to the Red Dead Wiki, if the player shoots at Irish before one of his missions, he either fires back or runs away, and he cannot be killed. After "We Shall Be Together in Paradise", he can be killed, but only by a very quick player, often with Dead Eye; otherwise he disappears for good. After "On Shaky's Ground", a player who catches up with his wagon and climbs back on makes him flee, and he can then be killed or hogtied.
According to the Red Dead Wiki, when Irish puts his revolver away in "We Shall Be Together in Paradise", the game plays a holstering animation, and the gun then reappears tucked in the front of his trousers.
"Think you're a real hard-case, don't you?", one of Irish's multiplayer taunts, is also shouted by Kent Gallaway, one of the gunmen at the Lake Don Julio cabin, if he survives a few seconds.
According to its mission page, "We Shall Be Together in Paradise" belongs to the New Austin missions but is counted as a Nuevo Paraíso mission in the game's statistics.
