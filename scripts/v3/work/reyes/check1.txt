
Abraham Reyes is the leader of the rebels of Nuevo Paraíso, in Mexico, in Red Dead Redemption (2010), set in 1911. The son of a nobleman, he leads a revolt against the province's governor, Colonel Agustin Allende, and the government of President Ignacio Sanchez. John Marston frees him from a firing squad at El Presidio after Luisa Fortuna tells him of his capture, and Reyes's rebels save John from his own execution in Chuparosa. In exchange for John's help, Reyes's spies track down Javier Escuella, and the rebels take El Presidio, then Escalera; Allende and Bill Williamson, fleeing by stagecoach, die on the road, shot by John or by Reyes.
In 1914, the last newspaper of the game, the Blackwater Ledger & Sentinel, reports that President Reyes denies being a tyrant after troops shot thousands of peaceful protesters south of Mexico City. He is voiced by Josh Segarra. He also appears in the non-canon expansion Undead Nightmare, as the undead source of the plague, whom John kills in the second-to-last mission.
Key facts
In "Must a Savior Die?", John shoots his executioner at El Presidio and rides him to Luisa.
In "Cowards Die Many Times", Reyes and his rebels save John from execution in Chuparosa, where De Santa has betrayed him.
In "The Great Mexican Train Robbery", he and John rob an army supply train in Chuparosa.
In "The Gates of El Presidio", his rebels storm the fort so that John can reach Javier Escuella.
In "An Appointed Time", the rebels take Escalera; Luisa is killed trying to save him, and Allende and Bill die on the road.
In 1914, the Blackwater Ledger & Sentinel calls him President Reyes.
In Undead Nightmare, which is not canon, John kills him as an undead in "A Civilized Man".

Biography
Origins
Red Dead Redemption gives no birth date for Abraham Reyes. His background comes from an article of the Blackwater Ledger, "Another Civil War in Mexico?", printed in issue No. 56. It describes him as a nobleman's son "born in a ranch just outside the capital", Mexico City. He was schooled in Mexico and in Europe. The paper adds that he was expelled from a Swiss boarding school "after an incident with a chamber maid". Neither of his parents is named. His mother is dead by 1911: Reyes says she would turn in her grave if he married a peasant girl. According to Luisa Fortuna, his family does not approve of her as a match for him.
Reyes himself tells John Marston a little more about his youth. On the ride to El Presidio, he says that when Javier Escuella was making a name for himself in the province, he was still travelling in Europe and living in Madrid with the ambassador's wife; he presents it as a time before his rebellious urges took hold. According to the Red Dead Wiki, he also says in "The Great Mexican Train Robbery" that his nursemaid used to tell him that one day everybody would know him; the line does not appear in the wiki's transcript of that mission.
The newspapers of 1911 set the political scene. Mexico is ruled by General Ignacio Sanchez, who, according to issue No. 53, "seized power last year in a bloody coup in which he killed his own brother". Issue No. 55 reports riots, train hijackings by rebel bands and government death squads, all played down by the government. Issue No. 56 then names Reyes as the leader of a band of rebels that has "seriously challenged government rule" in Nuevo Paraíso and the surrounding areas. He promises to "bring Mexico into the twentieth century, and improve conditions for the poor". The same article says he wants Mexico to play a leading part in world affairs, through an alliance with the Habsburg and Ottoman empires. The article closes on this assessment: "It is this foresight and intelligence that has endeared him to a large section of Mexico and which is bringing the country to the brink of bloody anarchy."
Red Dead Redemption (1911)
In 1911, agents of the Bureau of Investigation hold John Marston's wife and son to force him to hunt down his former companions Bill Williamson and Javier Escuella. The trail leads John to Nuevo Paraíso, a Mexican province governed from Escalera by Colonel Agustin Allende in the name of Sanchez. In the game, Reyes's rebels are based at Agave Viejo, a ranch in the Perdido region. John works for both sides at once: for Allende and Captain Vincente de Santa, and for Luisa Fortuna and the rebels. Government officers describe Reyes to him before they meet (see Missions). Reyes is on screen in five missions of the second act, all in 1911, three of which he gives himself.
Act II: Nuevo Paraíso
In "Must a Savior Die?", John finds Luisa crying in front of her family's burned house. Reyes was riding to meet her at Roca de Madera when an army patrol ambushed him. He is now held at El Presidio, an army fort to the north, and is about to be executed. Luisa drives John there in a coach. John climbs over a broken section of the wall and reaches a sniper's nest. In the courtyard, the executioner tells Reyes that he will pay for his treason and for wanting to overthrow Allende, then orders the firing squad to form and aim. John shoots him with a Rolling Block Rifle, kills the soldiers and cuts Reyes free. Reyes asks for a horse and rides out behind John. A rebel guide leads them to Luisa, who waits with friends at a landing on the San Luis river.
When John says that Luisa sent him, Reyes does not react to her name at first, then calls her the love of his life. Learning John's name, he says he had taken him for the American working for Allende and had planned to shoot him in the back. He then calls Luisa "Laura", and John corrects him. Reyes promises a reward: money, women, or Luisa if John wants her. John answers that he is only here for two men. Reyes celebrates his escape as if he had beaten a hundred soldiers, which John points out was his own work. At the river, Reyes greets Luisa and tells John that he is like a brother and that his people need such a man. He invites John to his ranch at Agave Viejo and promises to help him find his two men, if John helps win the people their freedom in return. He leaves with Luisa, singing to her and calling her Laura again.
In "Cowards Die Many Times", Allende tells John that Bill and Javier have been captured in Chuparosa, and De Santa takes him there. In the church, a soldier knocks John out. He comes to as his head is plunged into a horse trough, and De Santa's men prepare to execute him. Reyes and his rebels attack the town at that moment, and Reyes kills the executioner who is about to shoot John. He calls John over, cuts his ropes and tells him it was lucky that he arrived in time; John answers that they are now even. Reyes sends him to find his weapons. The rebels fight the soldiers in the streets, and John kills Captain Espinoza in the town hall. Bill and Javier were never in Chuparosa.
After the battle, Reyes gathers his rebels in the town and gives a speech. He tells them that the day has shown the regime's days are numbered, and promises a free republic justly governed by fine men. He warns them that the work is not finished, and that they will fight on, day and night, until local tyrants like Allende are gone and their followers put to the sword. He ends with cries of long live the revolution and long live Mexico, which the crowd takes up.
"The Great Mexican Train Robbery" begins at Agave Viejo. Reyes greets John as the American bandit turned bounty hunter who is about to win the Mexican revolution. He climbs on a stump to rouse his men, then tells John that their energy feeds him and that he and his people were sent to each other. He promises to be a fair and wise ruler because he knows the people and the land. When John answers with sarcasm, Reyes says sarcasm should be beneath a man like John and proposes a robbery for a good cause. On the ride, he asks John whether he has really come to Mexico to murder his two best friends. Pressed by John, he admits that he does not know exactly where Bill and Javier are, only that they are in Mexico, and that his best men are looking for them.
Reyes asks how John met the two men. John tells him about the gang of Dutch van der Linde, who robbed the rich, believed the system of power was rotten and thought change could only come through brutal means. Reyes asks whether Dutch was a revolutionary like him, says he likes the sound of this other violent idealist and asks where he is now; John answers that Dutch went insane. Reyes says John's story would make a good corrido, a Mexican ballad celebrating heroes, and that more than a hundred have been written about him. He adds that people in the capital already talk about his escape from El Presidio. He then describes Allende as a coward who enjoys beating his people, and as the puppet of Sanchez, a dictator who talks of reform while the people grow poorer.
The target is an army train full of supplies, about to leave the station in Chuparosa. Reyes explains the plan: his men will create a loud distraction while John boards the train and uncouples the army cars. He hands John a knife to kill the guards quietly. John clears the train, takes the engine and brings it to Reyes, who congratulates him and tells him to blow the hatch of the armored car. John cracks the safe inside and keeps its contents. Reyes then rides off to deliver the goods, telling John that his spies will soon have the information he wants. In the meantime, Luisa passes on a request from Reyes in "Father Abraham", and the rebels help John bring down Captain de Santa in "Captain De Santa's Downfall".
At the start of "The Gates of El Presidio", John walks in on Reyes having sex with a woman at Agave Viejo. When John asks what Luisa would say, Reyes first asks who he means. He then advises John to try her himself, and laughs at the idea that she thinks she is his wife. He says he will never marry a peasant: he is going to be the next president of Mexico, and his wife will meet ambassadors and kings. He then announces a present for John: they will bring the man John seeks to justice together.
On the wagon, Reyes has heard of the fight at Sepulcro and of De Santa's death. If John killed De Santa himself, Reyes says he is glad it was his bullet after Chuparosa. If John left him to the rebels, Reyes asks why he did not do it himself. Reyes then reveals that Javier is at El Presidio, the place where they first met, but that he knows nothing of Williamson. He has been told that the two men paid Allende to hide them and have been under his protection since John arrived in Mexico. Taking El Presidio, he says, will open the road to Escalera. When John describes Javier as a cynic who wanted to be a romantic, Reyes answers that John is the opposite.
The ride goes on. Asked who the woman was, Reyes calls her another loyal supporter of a free Mexico and gives no name; John retorts that he cannot even remember the name of the girl he is to marry. He tells John that a man like him cannot belong to one woman, and that he owes it to Mexico to father children and spread noble blood among the peasants. He lists the insults Americans use for Mexicans and calls John a fine socialist when John says he judges a man by how he is treated. Asked about the Chinese workers of the region, Reyes calls them an inferior race. He dreams aloud of storming Allende's mansion in Escalera and sleeping in his silk sheets. Asked what he will do with power once Mexico is his, he says he will give the greatest speech of his life, march on the capital against Sanchez, and delegate. He suggests that John could one day come back to Mexico, like Landon Ricketts, and join his staff.
Near the fort, Reyes explains that his men will launch a feint attack on a side entrance while John drives the wagon, loaded with five crates of TNT, at the main gate and jumps off before it hits. He lights the fuse straight away. The gate blows, the rebels pour in, and Reyes sends John to find Escuella. John catches Javier after a chase; depending on the player, he captures him or kills him. Reyes then calls John back: the army has sent reinforcements. John drives them off with a cannon, and Reyes declares that El Presidio belongs to the rebels. As John comes back for Javier, alive or dead, Reyes comments on the reunion of two old friends (see Quotes). John then hands Javier over to Edgar Ross and Archer Fordham near the border bridge; Reyes is not present.
"An Appointed Time" is the last mission of the second act. The army has executed rebels in Escalera and captured Reyes. John walks through the town as rebels fight the soldiers, and finds Reyes being beaten in the square by Major Raul Zubieta and his men before his execution. Reyes greets John with a mocking welcome to Mexico. John orders the soldiers to let him go and draws his gun. Reyes urges Zubieta to shoot, calling John a drunk. Luisa then rushes at the soldiers with a knife, shouting at them to release her man, and the soldiers guarding Reyes shoot her dead. John kills two of them. He offers to settle it with Zubieta there and then; Zubieta accepts, on condition that they fight like men, not like dogs. John kills him in a duel, then cuts Reyes loose.
Reyes declares that the people are ready and that they will overthrow the colonel that day. A rebel tells him that prisoners in Allende's jail will fight for them, and Reyes sends John to free his men. The rebels then storm the hill. Reyes has John take the machine gun while they blow the gate with gunpowder barrels, then has him defend the gate against soldiers coming up the slope. As the rebels break down the door of the mansion, Allende and Bill Williamson escape in an armored stagecoach. Reyes and John ride after them.
Once the coach is stopped, Allende forces Bill out and offers him to John in exchange for his own life. The player decides: John can shoot Allende, Bill or both. Reyes shoots whichever man John spares, and both men if John fires at neither. In the version given by the wiki's transcript, Reyes kills Allende after a last taunt from the colonel, and Bill is shot by Reyes or John as he reaches for Allende's gun. Reyes then proclaims that Allende is dead and that Mexico is his, and thanks John. John reminds him of the people who died for it, Luisa among them. Reyes cannot remember who she was, promises that a day will be named after her and calls it Laura's Day. Corrected, he says he also knew a Laura.
Reyes invites John to ride with the rebels on the capital. John declines: with Williamson dead, the men who hold his family want him back in Blackwater. Reyes says he will miss him. John doubts Reyes will even remember him, wishes him luck with the revolution and tells him to remember why he wanted power if he wins it. The two men part there, and Reyes does not appear again in the story. The end of the mission opens the West Elizabeth region.
Act III: West Elizabeth
While John hunts Dutch in West Elizabeth, the Blackwater Ledger reports the rest of Reyes's rise. Issue No. 58 announces that Allende, governor of Nuevo Paraíso, "was killed yesterday, following a fire fight with rebels". It adds: "Unsubstantiated reports suggest American mercenaries may have been fighting on both sides during the battle." Reyes calls it a great victory that will lead all the way to the Presidential Palace in Mexico City. He promises to bring greater political freedom to Nuevo Paraíso and to set up a workers council at once. He also releases a new state anthem, "a ballad of his own composition".
Two issues later, in No. 60, the article "Sanchez's Government Falls" reports that Reyes has taken power. After weeks of fighting around the capital, food riots and battles in many provinces, the Fifth Mexican Cavalry Division switched sides after Reyes promised to raise army pay and double leave. His supporters ransacked the president's palace. Sanchez is said to have fled into hiding, though "conflicting reports suggest he may have been killed". Reyes tells the paper: "Finally, my people, the people of this proud and valiant land are free. A new golden age for our country is just beginning." He promises a period of stability, then elections, and announces a three-day fiesta and a national holiday. Rioting goes on across much of the country, and early reports suggest that up to 100,000 people may have died in the conflict.
The 1914 newspaper
Reyes is still president in 1914. Issue No. 213 of the Blackwater Ledger & Sentinel, which can be read with Jack Marston after John's last mission, carries the article "President Reyes Denies He is a Tyrant, Despite Massacre". It opens on "last month's bloody massacre in a small town south of Mexico City, in which thousands were shot by troops while peacefully protesting the government". The article does not say who gave the order. Reyes denies rumors that he is a tyrant who has gone back on his promise to bring peace to Mexico. He says the path to democratic rule has been slower than he would have liked, but that progress is being made. He points to the new presidential palace as proof that Mexicans can work together, and says plans for a concert hall and other civic buildings remain. He also denies reports of widespread starvation, and says Mexico is about to enter a new golden age.
Undead Nightmare (non-canon)
Undead Nightmare, an expansion released in 2010, is not part of the series' canon. In it, Reyes is the source of the plague. In "Mother Superior Blues", the Mother Superior of Las Hermanas tells John that a woman linked the plague to something Reyes did, and sends him to Escalera, where he must find Reyes in the presidential mansion. In "A Civilized Man", the second-to-last mission of the expansion, John enters the mansion and finds an undead Reyes chasing a young woman around the dining table. John shouts at him to stop, and Reyes turns on him. He fights like a Bruiser, one of the heavy undead that charge the player, and John puts him down with a shot to the head.
The woman then explains that Reyes took a mask from the catacombs beneath the town, and that putting it back will end the plague. According to the wiki's Undead Nightmare page, she says his lust for invulnerability drove him to steal this ancient Aztec mask. According to the Red Dead Wiki's page on Reyes, he put it on in the belief that it would make him immortal, and it turned him into one of the undead instead. John takes the mask through the catacombs, whose entrance lies in the stands of the bullring behind the mansion, and puts it back on its altar. The woman reveals that she is Ayauhtéotl, an Aztec goddess, and vanishes. John returns to Beecher's Hope to find Abigail and Jack cured. Three months later, in the closing scene "On a Pale Horse", Seth Briars steals the mask from the catacombs, and the dead rise again.
Personality
The Red Dead Wiki quotes the Game of the Year Edition guide of Red Dead Redemption: "He is the 30-year-old son of a wealthy nobleman, who has seemingly sided with the peasants and the rebels to drive out the regime that supports Allende and de Santa. He is very vain and he does not seem to really care about the rights of the poor. However, Mr. Reyes does love to give speeches."
He addresses crowds of rebels from a stump or a town square and ends his speeches with cries of "Viva la revolución!". He calls John my brother, amigo or compadre. He exaggerates his own deeds when he retells them (see Quotes). He talks openly about the women he sleeps with and rarely names them. He forgets Luisa Fortuna's name more than once (see Relationships).
In his own words
On his plans, in "The Great Mexican Train Robbery": "When I rule these people, I will be fair and judicious and wise. How so you ask? Very simple... I know these people! I know this land. In short, I can make them better." Asked by John what he will do with power once Mexico is his, in "The Gates of El Presidio": "Like any great leader, my brother, I will delegate." On the Chinese workers, in the same mission: "That is different. They are an inferior race." John replies: "You have all the makings of a great leader, Abraham."
On Luisa, when John asks whether he will marry her: "Marry a peasant? My dear boy, don't be absurd. I'm going to be the next president of Mexico. My wife will meet ambassadors, kings, other great men. The very thought that I would marry some peasant girl with the hands of a farmer, well I really don't think so. [...]" On his women, a little later: "I owe it to the future of Mexico to breed, my brother. If I can get noble blood flowing through the veins of peasants, can you imagine how great this country can be? An army of Reyes! Of Kings!"
Names
Reyes is the Spanish word for kings, and he plays on it himself (see above). John calls him Abraham or Reyes; Luisa calls him Abraham or Abraham Reyes. He presents himself to John as "your brother, Abraham" when he tells him where Javier is hiding. Victor, one of his rebels, cheers "Viva Abraham Reyes!" when John shoots Captain de Santa himself at Sepulcro. The Blackwater Ledger calls him Mr. Reyes in 1911, and the Blackwater Ledger & Sentinel calls him President Reyes in 1914.
Appearance
He is a man of about thirty with straight black hair falling to his shoulders, dark eyes, a mustache and a short black beard on the chin. He usually wears a ragged green shawl around his shoulders over a brown jacket. According to the Red Dead Wiki, his belt buckle bears the arms of the Confederate States of America.
