Relationships
John Marston
Johnson agrees to help John against Bill Williamson in exchange for help with his own problems (see Biography). After the shootout at Pleasance House he compliments his aim and invites him to check in next time he is in town. When John says he does not want to be a policeman, Johnson answers: "Nor did I, my friend. I can promise you that." He suspects John of working for the government: "Well, I got a telegram from some federal clowns in Blackwater that says otherwise." John replies that he came because he was given no choice.
On the road to Tumbleweed, Johnson asks whether John is taking advantage of the MacFarlanes, then admits: "I trust you. It's just, all this business with Blackwater and Williamson and the past. I don't know...sometimes it's hard not to have doubts." The two men argue about the law: Johnson defends punishment and fixed rules, John says the law is not working (see In his own words above). After Fort Mercer, Johnson tells him: "I'm sorry about this, John." In Undead Nightmare, which is not canon, John offers to look for the missing deputies (see Biography).
Jonah
His deputy, who rides with him in all his missions. At John's first visit, Jonah takes him for one of Williamson's men and draws on him until the Marshal orders him to lower his gun and leave the room (see Biography). When Jonah needles John on the way to Pike's Basin, Johnson cuts him off: "Simmer down, Jonah." According to the Red Dead Wiki, Jonah wears a faded red shirt and a bowler hat and claims Irish heritage. In Undead Nightmare, which is not canon, Johnson gives John Jonah's sawed-off shotgun (see Biography).
Eli
His other deputy. Johnson sends him to spy on the rustlers at Pike's Basin and mocks his sarcasm and his lazy eye when Eli reports back (the line is under Quotes). Eli defends Nigel West Dickens and his tonic against the Marshal and John (see Nigel West Dickens below). At Fort Mercer, he calls for the gate to be opened for Dickens, and Johnson orders it opened and locked behind him. In Undead Nightmare, which is not canon, Eli and Jonah go missing in Armadillo (see Biography).
Priscilla Johnson
His wife, whom he never mentions in the game. According to the Red Dead Wiki, her tombstone stands in the cemetery of Coot's Chapel, to the left of the front entrance. It gives her date of death as April 23, 1903, and reads "Forever Smiling In Our Hearts". No cause of death is given.
Bonnie MacFarlane
In "Obstacles in Our Path", Bonnie advises John to pay the Marshal a visit in Armadillo about Bill Williamson. In "Women and Cattle", she has heard from Johnson that John plans to settle in the area. When Bill's gang kidnaps her, Johnson leads the posse to Tumbleweed (see Biography). According to the Red Dead Wiki, he takes her back to Armadillo afterward. For her mention of him in Red Dead Online, see Other media.
Drew MacFarlane
Bonnie's father comes to the Marshal's office when she disappears and accuses John (see Biography). Johnson tries to calm him: "Now Drew, nobody's lost anything yet." On the road, he tells John that the MacFarlanes have been through a lot and are vulnerable in different ways.
Bill Williamson
At their first meeting, Johnson tells John that Williamson is outside his jurisdiction, in the next county, and that his gang has kept away from Armadillo. Bill's men raid Ridgewood Farm and take Bonnie hostage to free Norman Deek (see Biography). Before the assault on Fort Mercer, Johnson calls Bill a cold-blooded killer (the line is under Quotes). Bill has already left for Mexico when the posse takes the fort (see Biography).
Norman Deek
Williamson's second-in-command, captured by Johnson's posse after the raid on Ridgewood Farm (see Biography). Johnson calls him "a glorified errand boy" and says: "Deek's the kind of man who's mean enough to be second-in-command but too cowardly and stupid to ever be a leader." Tied to a horse on the way to Tumbleweed, Deek threatens him: "You wait, Marshal. I'll be back for you." According to the Red Dead Wiki, Deek, like Johnson, wears a gun belt with two holsters.
Walton Lowe
The leader of a gang of road agents who rob the stagecoaches around Armadillo. When Walton rides out of the saloon, Johnson points him out ("There's the dumb rat-bastard now.") and follows him to Pleasance House (see Biography). He refuses to shoot him on sight: "Because that ain't how the law works." He adds: "And alive, he can still talk." According to the Red Dead Wiki, Walton shouts to his men that whoever kills the Marshal keeps his badge.
Nigel West Dickens
When Eli reports Dickens missing in "Justice in Pike's Basin", the Marshal dismisses him as a peddler of narcotics who cheats housewives, and complains that the town cares more about him than about the gang just wiped out (both lines are under Quotes). In "Spare the Rod, Spoil the Bandit", he settles on: "He's certainly a character, that West Dickens." At Fort Mercer, Dickens drives the wagon that gets John inside while Johnson and his deputies wait for the shooting to start (see Biography).
Seth Briars
Seth is part of the Fort Mercer plan and is already inside the fort when Johnson's posse waits on the hill (see Biography). The two men share no dialogue in the game.
Irish
Irish supplied the Gatling gun used at Fort Mercer but arrives once the fighting is over (see Biography). He and Johnson share no dialogue in the game.
Jake
The local guide Johnson hires to take John from Armadillo to Fort Mercer in "Exodus in America". On the ride, Jake tells John who sent him (see Names and title above).
The people of Armadillo
After the raid on Ridgewood Farm, a survivor accuses the Marshal and his men of failing to protect them: "You're just some man on a government payroll, taking money that the rest of us have to pay for with our lives!" According to the Red Dead Wiki, many townspeople speak ill of him. Baxter Deaton spreads rumors that "Marshal Johnson's buried stolen goods out at Río del Lobo" and that "the gang at Twin Rocks is in cahoots with Marshal Johnson", though he admits that most of his stories are made up. Mel Thaxton, a lawman stationed in Armadillo, complains that "The Marshal's an idiot, that's this place's problem." The wiki adds that Jeb Murphy, Eldin Grubb and Herbert Moon criticize him, and that people at MacFarlane's Ranch say he has the whole world on his shoulders.
Law enforcement, weapons and horses
A marshal's methods
Johnson works from the sheriff's office in Armadillo, where he keeps prisoners in the cells and takes telegrams and telephone calls; he gives his line as Armadillo 731. He rides out with his deputies Jonah and Eli and, when needed, a larger posse. He sends Eli to watch the canyon where the rustlers work. He hires Jake, the local guide who takes John from Armadillo to Fort Mercer at the start of the game. He has prisoners hogtied and brought back to his jail, and offers a 50-dollar reward to the man who kills the leader of the Ridgewood raiders (see Biography).
In the game
Johnson gives five story missions in the first act of Red Dead Redemption and one side mission in Undead Nightmare; the Missions table lists them. At Pike's Basin, the player can follow the deputies or stay with the Marshal and cover him. According to the Red Dead Wiki, he can be killed after "Hanging Bonnie MacFarlane": if John uses Dead Eye on the horses as Johnson and Bonnie ride away, both fall to the ground. Kotaku (Owen Good, August 17, 2010) gave the same tip: shoot his horse after that mission and he walks away on foot. In Undead Nightmare, which is not canon, he stays in his office after "Dinner for Two", making calls and doing paperwork according to the wiki, and he can be shot through the window.
Weapons and horses
He wears a gun belt with two holsters and a pair of nickel-plated Cattleman Revolvers. According to the Red Dead Wiki, he fires both only in cutscenes and a single one during gameplay; the "Dinner for Two" mission page also notes that he uses one revolver there. The wiki lists a Repeater Carbine, a Winchester Repeater and a Double-barreled Shotgun among his weapons. In Undead Nightmare, he hands John the Sawed-off Shotgun of his deputy Jonah (see Biography). The Red Dead Wiki gives him a Standardbred Pinto as his mount, and its trivia adds that he rides a Tersk in "Political Realities in Armadillo" and dismounts from a Kentucky Saddler in "The Assault on Fort Mercer".
Missions
Leigh Johnson has a strand of five story missions in the first act of Red Dead Redemption, all in New Austin, numbered 8, 9, 11, 23 and 24 in the story order. The first, "Political Realities in Armadillo", becomes available after Bonnie MacFarlane's "Obstacles in Our Path". It introduces cover, bounty hunting and fast travel from a campsite, and unlocks the bounty hunting jobs and the stranger missions "American Appetites" (part 1) and "Water and Honesty". "Justice in Pike's Basin" introduces basic ranged combat, opens the Pike's Basin gang hideout and unlocks Nigel West Dickens's strand. "Spare the Rod, Spoil the Bandit" requires "Women and Cattle" and "Old Swindler Blues"; "Hanging Bonnie MacFarlane" requires "The Burning". "The Assault on Fort Mercer", the last mission of New Austin, requires the strands of Johnson, Dickens, Seth Briars and Irish, introduces mounted weapons and the Gatling gun, and leads to "We Shall Be Together in Paradise", the first mission in Mexico. In Undead Nightmare, Johnson gives one side mission.

Development and performance
Casting
Leigh Johnson appears in Red Dead Redemption and its expansion Undead Nightmare, both released in 2010. Wikipedia credits Anthony De Longis with the role in both games. According to Wikipedia, De Longis is an American actor, stuntman and fight choreographer, born on March 23, 1950 in Glendale, California. His other roles include Blade in the film Masters of the Universe (1987) and the Kazon leader Jal Culluh in Star Trek: Voyager. A weapons master, he taught Michelle Pfeiffer the bullwhip for Batman Returns (1992) and gave Harrison Ford whip training for Indiana Jones and the Kingdom of the Crystal Skull (2008). No source consulted names a performer for Johnson's motion capture.
A first actor replaced
On August 17, 2010, Kotaku (Owen Good) relayed an account by the actor Colin Nissan, written for the blog of The Paris Review under the title "Searching For Me In Red Dead Redemption". Nissan had been cast as the Marshal and recorded his lines over two hours at Rockstar's studio in SoHo. According to the article, the Marshal appeared in a trailer two months before the game's release without Nissan's voice. When the game came out, Nissan found that the character spoke with another actor's voice.
Reception
Character lists
In a list of the ten best characters of the Red Dead Redemption franchise, published by Sportskeeda (page last modified on August 13, 2023), Aniket Mukherjee ranked Marshal Leigh Johnson seventh, between Seth Briars (eighth) and Nigel West Dickens (sixth). The author cited "his commitment to maintaining law and order in a chaotic frontier" and wrote that "His partnership with John Marston adds an interesting dynamic to the story." No other review or article dedicated to the character was found.
Other media and legacy
The Man from Blackwater (2010)
Johnson appears in Red Dead Redemption: The Man from Blackwater, a short film made with the game's engine, directed by John Hillcoat and broadcast by Fox on May 29, 2010, then by 5USA in the United Kingdom on June 5, 2010. The film follows the first act, in which John teams up with the marshal and his deputies, Nigel West Dickens and Seth Briars to take Fort Mercer. It reuses his first meeting with John and Jonah from "Political Realities in Armadillo", and the Ridgewood Farm massacre and the confrontation with Williamson from "Spare the Rod, Spoil the Bandit". When John explains his situation to Johnson, the film adds scenes that are not in the game, showing John's years in Dutch van der Linde's gang, among them a robbery of the Blackwater bank with Bill and Javier Escuella.
Multiplayer
Johnson became a playable character model in the multiplayer mode of Red Dead Redemption with the Liars and Cheats Pack, detailed by Rockstar on August 27, 2010 and released on September 21, 2010. Players select him in the Redemption section of the Outfitter, alongside John, Abigail, Jack, Bonnie MacFarlane, Luisa Fortuna, Nigel West Dickens and Seth Briars. The same pack added Grand Prix horse races; the third race of the Carreras Perdido Cup is named L. Johnson's Run, and according to the Red Dead Wiki it starts and ends in Armadillo. The versions of Red Dead Redemption released on PlayStation 4 and Nintendo Switch on August 17, 2023, then on PC on October 29, 2024, do not include the multiplayer mode.
Marketing
Rockstar released official artwork of Johnson for Red Dead Redemption. According to the Red Dead Wiki, a promotional image shows him crumpling a bounty notice in his hand, on which the hideout of the target, Campo Mirada, can be made out.
Red Dead Redemption 2 and Red Dead Online
Johnson does not appear in Red Dead Redemption 2. In its 1907 epilogue, Armadillo, emptied by a cholera outbreak, has a sheriff named Palmer, who resigns or is shot by the Del Lobo Gang depending on the player's intervention. According to the Red Dead Wiki, the game mentions briefly that Johnson is away because of the cholera outbreak, like most of the town's residents; the 1914 article mentions no break in his 17 years of service. Also according to the wiki, in Red Dead Online, set in 1898, Bonnie MacFarlane mentions him when she starts a recovery mission, saying she hopes he can come back as he is away because of the cholera outbreak.
Trivia
Red Dead Redemption
The in-game texts spell his title "Marshall" with a double l: his wife's tombstone at Coot's Chapel, the 1911 article "Shoot Out at Fort Mercer" and the 1914 headline "Marshall Johnson of Armadillo Retires".
His exact office is not consistent. According to the Red Dead Wiki, he wears a United States Marshal badge. In "Political Realities in Armadillo", he says Williamson is outside his jurisdiction because Fort Mercer lies in the next county. The 1914 article calls him a town marshal. His missions nonetheless take him to Pike's Basin, to Tumbleweed in Gaptooth Ridge and to Fort Mercer in Rio Bravo.
Many people in Armadillo speak badly of him. According to the quote list of his own page on the Red Dead Wiki, the Armadillo bank teller Baxter Deaton says "They say that Marshal Johnson's buried stolen goods out at Río del Lobo" and "I heard the gang at Twin Rocks is in cahoots with Marshal Johnson"; he also accuses an unnamed marshal of beating women. The same page notes that Deaton admits most of his rumors are made up. The quote list of Mel Thaxton includes "The Marshal's an idiot, that's this place's problem", a complaint heard in the saloon in 1911 and in 1914. According to the Red Dead Wiki, Jeb Murphy, Eldin Grubb and Herbert Moon also run him down, a man named Jake says he is not the man he used to be, and workers at MacFarlane's Ranch say he carries the whole world on his shoulders.
According to the mission page of "Hanging Bonnie MacFarlane", the player can follow Johnson and Bonnie when they ride off at the end: the marshal stops with her at a small camp near Del Lobo Rock, and neither can be spoken to. According to the character page, killing their horses in Dead Eye mode right after the mission makes them both fall, and both can then be killed.
According to the mission page of "The Assault on Fort Mercer", on rare occasions a cougar kills one of the lawmen during the ride to the fort, which fails the mission; it happens mostly at night.
According to the Red Dead Wiki, his gun belt carries two holsters, a feature shared in the game only with Norman Deek, Enepay and Benton Manning, and his outfit resembles the one worn by Sheriff O'Grady in Red Dead Revolver.
Undead Nightmare (non-canon)
After "Dinner for Two", Johnson stays in his office, where he can be seen making calls and doing paperwork. According to the mission page, he can be killed, for example with the shotgun he gave John, fired through the window close to his head.
According to the mission page, Ayauhtéotl can be seen outside the window nearest the marshal's desk at the start of the opening cutscene of "Dinner for Two".
